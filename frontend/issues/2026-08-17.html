ChatGPT’s Computer History tracks your clicks and keystrokes

OpenAI has launched a new opt-in feature called Computer History for ChatGPT's macOS desktop app that tracks users' clicks and keystrokes to build a timeline of their activity. The feature turns user actions into training data, enabling ChatGPT and Codex to learn how users work, suggest automations, and resume unfinished tasks. Unlike Microsoft's Windows Recall, which relied on screenshots, Computer History captures 'events' rather than images, video, or audio. Users can exclude certain apps and websites, delete specific entries, and the feature automatically ignores content in private or incognito browser tabs.

Why it matters

This is a significant and somewhat unsettling development in the AI assistant space. While the opt-in nature and granular controls are welcome compared to Microsoft's initially botched Recall rollout, the fundamental concept of an AI company tracking every click and keystroke on your computer raises serious privacy concerns.
