v2.1.234

Claude to start watermarking AI-generated text – but will it make quality worse?

Anthropic announced it will watermark AI-generated text from its Claude chatbot to comply with an upcoming EU regulation requiring all AI-generated text to be watermarked by December 2026. The watermark works by altering the small, random word choices the model makes during text generation, leaving a pattern detectable by Anthropic and those with a decoding key. Tech blogger John Gruber criticized the move, arguing it would constrain Claude's word choices and degrade writing quality. However, UCL computer science professor Steven Murdoch said the change would likely have no noticeable impact, noting that LLMs already incorporate randomness in their word selection as an essential part of how they function.

DNA's extraordinary information density—capable of storing about 215 million gigabytes per gram—is leveraged alongside perovskite's electronic properties to create a system that can store and process information in the same location, similar to how neurons function in the brain. Unlike ordinary resistors, memristors retain a record of previous electrical activity even after power is removed. Whisker’s AI-powered litter robot thinks my cats swapped bodies

The Verge reviews the $899 Litter-Robot 5 Pro from Whisker, which features AI-powered cat facial recognition to track individual cats' litter box usage for health insights. While the reviewer praises the automatic scooping functionality, the AI cat identification system fails significantly, confusing the reviewer's two cats and essentially thinking they swapped bodies. Why it matters

Amazon, which started off selling books, is destroying rare texts to train AI

According to a 404 Media investigation, Amazon is purchasing rare and out-of-print books, cutting off their spines, and scanning them at a Las Vegas facility (known as VGT3) to use as training data for its large language models. Rare books are particularly valuable for AI training because they contain text not already available on the internet and are guaranteed not to be AI-generated, helping avoid 'model collapse' — a degradation in LLM output quality that occurs when models train on too much AI-generated text. Amazon confirmed it 'purchases books through commercial channels to improve the products and services customers use.' The article also notes that other companies like Anthropic have faced scrutiny for using illegally pirated books for AI training.

Why it matters

There is a deep irony in Amazon — a company that began as an online bookstore championing access to books — now physically destroying rare texts for AI training data. This practice raises serious concerns about cultural preservation. Rare and out-of-print books are irreplaceable artifacts of human knowledge and creativity, and destroying them for marginal improvements in AI model performance represents a troubling prioritization of corporate interests over cultural heritage.
