MIT Tech Review AI

'Reverse game theory' proposed as new approach to keeping AI agents in check

MIT Technology Review reports on mechanism design — 'reverse game theory' — as an alignment approach where external incentive structures make it rational for AI agents to behave well regardless of their internal states. Researchers including MIT economist Stephen Morris and Princeton's Elad Hazan propose techniques like cross-checking models against each other, competitive pitting, and pairing oppositely-biased agents.

Why it matters

A genuinely interesting intellectual framework that sidesteps the hard problem of interpretability, though it assumes AI agents are rational optimizers — an assumption that may not hold for all failure modes.

The Rundown AI

MIT shows GPT-6 Astra can improve other AI models by directly rewriting their weights

MIT researchers published a study showing OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra can improve other AI models by directly rewriting their internal weights without retraining. In one test, modifying just 0.016% of Alibaba's Qwen model's weights fixed a repetition bug and boosted its math score from 7.9% to 48.1%. The technique was less effective on Google's Gemma 3. Separately, OpenAI introduced LifeVal, a benchmark for everyday tasks where GPT-6 Astra scored 80.6% on average and 100% on 10 of 16 tasks.

Why it matters

The weight-editing result is genuinely novel and raises profound questions about AI models modifying each other — both exciting for debugging and alarming for safety. The LifeVal benchmark feels tacked on.

The Verge AI

Anthropic updates Claude Code to prevent users from cloning specific video games

Anthropic has updated Claude Code's system prompt on Opus 5.5 to prevent users from recreating specific existing video games, citing its usage policy against IP infringement. Users can still create original games, genre-based games, and games from their own designs. Anthropic's Addy Osmani acknowledged the update and said the company will iterate on the balance.

Why it matters

A reasonable IP guardrail, though the line between 'clone' and 'genre-based game inspired by' will be hard to enforce consistently.

Guardian AI

UK prosecutor cites AI-fabricated case in high court child rape proceedings

A senior Crown Prosecution Service prosecutor, Ravinder Dhillon, cited a completely fabricated legal precedent in a high court case involving alleged child rape. The nonexistent case was generated by an AI search tool, adding to a growing pattern of dozens of AI-related errors in UK court proceedings.

Why it matters

A deeply troubling incident in one of the most serious possible legal contexts. The justice system's failure to mandate verification of AI-sourced citations is becoming indefensible.

MIT Tech Review AI

Trump's 'Super Intelligence' rebrand of AI meets uneven government adoption one month in

One month after Trump signed an executive order directing agencies to rebrand 'artificial intelligence' as 'Super Intelligence,' adoption remains inconsistent. The term, which won an X poll over alternatives like 'Extreme Intelligence,' is appearing in government websites and ICE surveillance solicitations, but the Pentagon, FTC, and HHS have stumbled or ignored the change. The article frames this as a political loyalty test in the pattern of other Trump-era rebrands.

Why it matters

The rebrand is transparently a loyalty-signaling mechanism rather than a substantive policy change, but its enforcement dynamics reveal how executive power works through language.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre warns that AI-powered malware is 'highly likely' to be used in cyberattacks against critical national infrastructure — including power stations, airports, and hospitals — by 2028, expecting AI to enable ransomware attacks on these systems within two years.

Why it matters

A credible warning from a respected agency, though the 'highly likely by 2028' framing is vague enough to be unfalsifiable in the near term.

MIT Tech Review AI

Only 48% of AI agent-generated insights lead to business-improving actions, report finds

A MIT Technology Review report sponsored by Snowflake finds only 48% of agent-generated insights currently lead to business-improving actions (up from 37% a year ago). The primary culprit is insufficient business context (55% of executives), followed by fragmented data (45%). 'Insight leaders' achieving over 70% actionable insights invest in semantic layers and AI governance rather than bigger models. 52% of organizations report 'insight overload' from agents.

Why it matters

Useful data points buried in a vendor-sponsored report. The 'insight overload' finding — agents generating more noise than signal — is the most interesting takeaway.

TechCrunch AI

Apple Watch Series 12 'Audio Intelligence' features enter beta testing

Apple is beta testing 'Audio Intelligence' features for Apple Watch Series 12 in iOS 27.3, including sound recognition for deaf/hard-of-hearing users, Shazam song ID, 'Live Rewind' (capturing the last 15 seconds of conversation as text via double-pressing the digital crown), and 'Siri Recaps' (bullet-point conversation summaries). Privacy signals include audible chimes, visual animations, and auto-deletion of snippets after 30 seconds if not saved. The beta launched via Apple India's press release.

Why it matters

Live Rewind and Siri Recaps are genuinely useful accessibility and productivity features, and Apple's privacy-first design with chimes and auto-delete is the right approach for always-listening wearables.

The Verge AI

New York Times and other publishers sue Brave for AI scraping and paywall bypassing

The New York Times, New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and Orlando Sentinel have sued Brave Software, alleging it unlawfully scraped their content to build the Brave Index, which was sold to Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere for model training. The lawsuits also target Brave's paywall-blocker feature. Publishers claim Brave never sought permission, never compensated them, and circumvented access-blocking measures.

Why it matters

A significant legal escalation that opens a new front in the AI copyright wars — targeting the intermediary data broker rather than the AI companies directly.
