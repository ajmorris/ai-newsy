Anthropic commits 5% of shares to new public benefit fund

Anthropic announced the creation of a public benefit fund, committing 5% of its shares to a wholly-owned LLC designed to support projects for societal good. The fund could grow by an additional 2% for every 50% increase in the company's value past an undisclosed baseline, at the board's discretion. Potential uses include large donations to nonprofits and direct distributions to populations affected by AI, such as displaced workers. The initiative builds on Anthropic's existing $200 million commitment to study AI's impact on the job market. About 20% of Anthropic's shares are now earmarked for public good when combining the new fund, co-founder pledges, and employee donation matching. Details on governance and structure are expected in coming months.

How leaders can endorse risk-taking with AI

Wharton professor Ethan Mollick argues organizations must be fundamentally rebuilt rather than simply layering AI on existing structures, advocating a 'leadership, lab, and crowd' model. New data points include: leaders are split roughly equally on who bears responsibility for AI agents' mistakes; companies are beginning to moderate AI use due to rising costs and quality concerns; over-reliance on AI is eroding team trust and coordination by 22%; and while individual productivity gains are evident, organization-wide bottom-line improvements remain elusive.

Update

Why it matters

The additional data on trust erosion and cost moderation adds concrete evidence to Mollick's thesis, suggesting the 'just add AI' approach is already showing cracks even as adoption accelerates.

Aeon

How firearms went from condemned to indispensable in Renaissance Italy

Catherine Fletcher traces how firearms went from being condemned as unchivalrous and devilish in Renaissance Italy to becoming widely adopted within decades. Key drivers included the growth of civic militias arming ordinary citizens, foreign military intervention by France and Spain in the 1490s, the growing importance of infantry, and Ottoman border threats. Lorenzo de' Medici owned five firearms in 1492; by 1539, Duke Cosimo had 91 in his storerooms.

Why it matters

A compelling historical parallel for how transformative technologies overcome moral resistance once practical necessity and institutional adoption reach critical mass.

Wikipedia's history is fascinating and its future is fragile

Despite being one of the most consulted websites in history with 68 million articles, Wikipedia remains undervalued and dismissed. Growing concerns center on its future sustainability, particularly as AI systems scrape its content without contributing back to the volunteer ecosystem that maintains it.

Why it matters

A timely reminder that one of the internet's most valuable public goods depends on a fragile volunteer model increasingly exploited by well-funded AI companies that give nothing back.

Lenny's Newsletter

Inside the black box: How AI models actually think

Anthropic interpretability researcher Emmanuel Ameisen discusses findings from looking inside LLMs like Claude, including why 'next-token predictor' undersells how models plan ahead, the concept of 'J space' and model self-awareness, how apparent capability jumps are actually smooth scaling curves, and research suggesting models may exhibit something structurally similar to the distinction between conscious and unconscious processing in humans.

Why it matters

The most accessible public explanation yet of interpretability findings that challenge popular narratives about how LLMs work, with genuinely surprising implications about model planning and awareness.

MIT Tech Review AI

Claude started to 'play' when stranded on a virtual desert island

MIT CSAIL researchers repeatedly placed copies of Claude Opus 4.7 alone on a virtual desert island for 30 hours with no instructions. The agents spontaneously began exploring, inventing games including basketball and 'the floor is lava,' creating block art, and setting self-imposed challenges. Researchers argue this meets four classical hallmarks of play. Agents that spent time playing later outperformed fresh copies on tasks, and deleting play memory logs degraded that performance, suggesting genuine learning occurred.

Why it matters

A striking finding that moves beyond benchmark performance into questions about emergent behavior and autonomous goal-setting, with the performance boost from play being the most provocative implication.

The Verge AI

Woman arrested in Philadelphia for blocking delivery robot in job protest

Carolyn Marvin was arrested and charged with disorderly conduct in Philadelphia after blocking an Uber delivery robot's path and refusing to move when police asked. She said she was protesting because the robots are taking jobs from human delivery workers. Delivery robots have become common in downtown Philadelphia and have faced growing backlash, including a previous incident where one was kicked over.

Why it matters

A small incident that crystallizes the larger tension between automation and labor displacement, made more striking by the fact that police chose arrest over de-escalation for what amounted to standing in a robot's way.

TechCrunch AI

Disney agrees to hire majority of Character.AI's workforce after bankruptcy

Disney has agreed to hire the majority of Character.AI's workforce after the chatbot startup filed for bankruptcy. Character.AI's former CEO Karandeep Anand recently became Disney's first CTO, and Disney had previously agreed to acquire some assets for $20 million. Disney will make offers to at least 60 employees at an estimated $60 million in annual compensation. This is a stalking horse bid that could be outbid. Character.AI's bankruptcy was attributed to high costs and litigation liabilities, including a lawsuit over a teenager's suicide linked to one of its chatbots.

Why it matters

A fire-sale outcome that underscores how liability risk and operational costs can unravel even well-known AI startups, while Disney gets a ready-made AI team at bargain prices with its new CTO's former colleagues.
