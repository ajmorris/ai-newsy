Engram is a sampler that turns broken AI hallucinations into music

Thoughtful Things has launched a Kickstarter for Engram, a hardware sampler and groovebox that runs a small, locally-hosted AI audio model to mangle and hallucinate new sounds from incoming audio. Unlike generative AI music services like Suno, Engram is designed as an experimental instrument for sound design rather than a finished-song generator. It runs offline with a custom-trained model built on openly licensed audio datasets, and the company plans to open-source the firmware so users can load their own models. The device is described as a 'field recorder for latent space,' drawing inspiration from circuit bending by letting musicians intentionally push and break tiny AI models to produce glitchy, uncanny textures.

Why it matters

This is one of the more thoughtful and genuinely interesting applications of AI in music I've seen. By deliberately embracing the weird, broken edges of small audio models rather than trying to produce polished output, Engram sidesteps the ethical and creative concerns that plague tools like Suno and Udio.
