Planned Amazon data center could become the biggest climate polluter in the U.S.

Amazon is planning a data center in Pecos County, Texas, with an on-site natural gas power plant permitted to release 33 million tons of carbon dioxide per year, which would make it the largest single source of climate pollution in the United States. An Amazon spokesperson confirmed the facility will use on-site power generation and won't raise electricity costs for Texas families. Amazon's carbon emissions were already up 16% last year due to AI demands, moving away from its 2040 carbon elimination pledge. The spokesperson acknowledged 'the world looks different now' compared to when the company co-founded the climate pledge, while claiming their commitment hasn't changed.

This story is deeply troubling and represents a stark illustration of how the AI boom is directly undermining climate commitments. An Amazon data center could have the worst polluting power plant in the country

Amazon is investing in a new gas-burning power plant in Pecos County, West Texas, to power a new data center. The plant, called GW Ranch, features 35 natural-gas turbines producing 7.65 gigawatts of electricity and has received a Texas permit allowing up to 33 million tons of CO2 emissions — more than even the largest coal plant in the US. The plant would initially not be connected to the state's power grid, instead delivering power primarily to Amazon's data center. While plants rarely emit as much as their permits allow, the scale of permitted pollution is notable. The development reflects a broader trend of tech companies like Amazon, Meta, and Google building dedicated power infrastructure to meet surging energy demands from AI and data centers.

Why it matters

This story highlights a deeply troubling contradiction at the heart of Big Tech's climate commitments. Amazon and its peers have made bold pledges about sustainability and carbon neutrality, yet here we see Amazon effectively commissioning what could be the most polluting single power plant in the country — solely to feed the insatiable energy appetite of AI-driven data centers. The NextSlide team is now working on ChatGPT. Financial terms were not disclosed, and Beshry noted the acquisition actually took place earlier in 2026. Guardian AI

Parenting is hard. Should we let AI do it for us? | Dave Schilling

The article by Dave Schilling critiques the growing trend of tech companies marketing AI-powered parenting tools—such as eye-movement trackers, personalized podcasts, and 'optimized nap predictions'—to parents. Schilling humorously highlights the absurdity and expense of these products, questioning whether parents actually need AI to help raise their children, while reflecting on the already costly and demanding nature of parenting.

Why it matters

The article appears to take a skeptical and satirical stance toward the tech industry's push to insert AI into parenting. Schilling seems to argue that these products are unnecessary, driven more by corporate profit motives than genuine parental need, and that the human, imperfect aspects of parenting shouldn't be 'optimized' away by technology. The tone is humorous but carries a serious underlying concern about the over-technologization of family life.
