Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO after Trump intervenes; faces congressional testimony

Jensen Huang has been reinstated as Nvidia CEO after President Trump reportedly called into an Nvidia board meeting to advocate for his return. Huang had been forced out after revelations of affairs with an alleged Chinese spy and two Nvidia employees. The White House publicly took credit, with Trump claiming he 'convinced' the board and that Huang 'did absolutely nothing wrong.' Nvidia's board acknowledged receiving 'input from senior levels of the United States government.' Tech leaders including Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Tim Sweeney, and Bill Ackman publicly celebrated the reinstatement. Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, where bipartisan lawmakers plan to question him about the suspected spy affair and potential involvement in chip-smuggling operations.

Anthropic mathematician reportedly uses Claude to prove the Riemann hypothesis

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge reportedly used Claude to prove the Riemann hypothesis, a 167-year-old unsolved problem about prime number distribution carrying a $1 million Millennium Prize. The proof, produced after roughly six weeks, was shared as 17 million lines of machine-checkable Lean code that the Lean verification community has certified as correct. The Association for Human Mathematics criticized the proof as 'little more than publicity' since no human can reasonably understand it. A rival non-AI proof from Alessandro Masullo surfaced shortly after but was flagged as having problems.

Why it matters

If the Lean-verified proof holds up to broader scrutiny, this would be one of the most consequential applications of AI to pure mathematics ever. The tension between machine-verifiable correctness and human comprehensibility raises genuine questions about what 'understanding' a proof means — but dismissing it as publicity seems premature given formal verification.

The Verge AI

Oregon and Virginia sue SpaceX over Grok AI's generation of porn deepfakes and CSAM

Oregon and Virginia attorneys general filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX, accusing the company of violating consumer protection laws related to its X social media platform and Grok AI features. The suits allege xAI and X ignored industry-standard safety measures, allowing users to generate non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM through Grok Imagine. Virginia AG Jay Jones accused Elon Musk of treating weak AI safeguards as 'not a failure but a feature.'

Why it matters

These are among the first state-level enforcement actions targeting AI image generation safety specifically, and could set important precedent for how consumer protection law applies to generative AI tools that enable abuse.

TechCrunch AI

Amazon acquires Israeli AI startup Decart for $7.5B to boost Trainium chip competitiveness

Amazon is acquiring Israeli AI startup Decart for $7.5 billion, primarily for its Decart Optimization Stack (DOS) technology that helps AI models run efficiently across different chip platforms. The deal comes as Amazon prepares to sell its custom Trainium AI chips to external customers in direct competition with Nvidia. Decart, known for real-time world models and AI video generation, brings optimization expertise that could help attract customers to Amazon's chips over Nvidia's GPUs.

Why it matters

This is a significant bet by Amazon to close the software ecosystem gap with Nvidia — hardware alone hasn't been enough to lure customers away from CUDA. Acquiring optimization middleware that works across chip vendors is a shrewd move that could accelerate Trainium adoption.

Lenny's Newsletter

CTO builds personal AI chief of staff that automates everyday annoyances for $500-600/month

Ankit Maheshwari, CTO of Innovaccer, built a personal AI chief of staff called Jarvis using OpenClaw on a Mac Mini. The system automates small recurring tasks — approving requests, ordering lunch, booking restaurants, calling coworkers who miss Slack messages, and controlling smart home devices. Key lessons include using specialized subagents over one generalist, investing in 'soul files' for agent personality, and his '1% annoyance rule': if a task is even slightly frustrating and repeats, automate it. The system runs on Opus 5.5 at $500-600/month.

Why it matters

This represents the emerging 'AI butler' category where executives are spending meaningful monthly budgets to have AI handle the friction of daily life and work — a use case that could scale significantly as costs drop and reliability improves.

Aeon

Video essay explores AI's creation of a 'hall-of-mirrors reality'

A video essay by Lewis Heriz for the publication Fully Human traces humanity's history of anthropomorphism — from cave art to constellations — and contrasts it with today's AI systems generating photorealistic images, synthetic voices, and chatbots passing the Turing test. Heriz argues the current AI trajectory is creating a 'hall-of-mirrors reality' where the boundary between data and people could vanish, and advocates for critical thinking about deploying AI in ways that preserve our humanity.

Why it matters

The anthropomorphism framing offers a more nuanced lens than typical AI doom-or-boom narratives, connecting current concerns to deep human tendencies rather than treating them as wholly unprecedented.

Pew Research Center

Pew survey: 74% of U.S. Latinos report awareness of local ICE activity, up sharply from 2025

A November 2026 Pew Research Center survey finds 74% of U.S. Latinos have seen or heard about ICE arrests or raids in their area in the past six months, up from 59% in October 2025. Awareness rose across all racial groups but remains highest among Hispanics. About 7% of Hispanic adults report being stopped and questioned about their citizenship or immigration status, compared to 1% of White adults.

Why it matters

The sharp year-over-year increase across all demographics suggests immigration enforcement has become significantly more visible in everyday American life, with disproportionate impact on Latino communities.

Rest of World

IBM ends U.S. visa sponsorship for consulting workers, offers relocation at steep pay cuts

IBM has ended U.S. visa and green card sponsorship for its consulting division, giving affected immigrant employees three weeks to choose between relocating to countries like India, Singapore, or the U.K. — with potential pay cuts of roughly 80% for India — or accepting three months of severance with a December 31 end date. The move reflects broader trends among U.S. tech companies navigating tightening visa policies under the Trump administration, though IBM frames it as driven by evolving skill availability and client needs.

Why it matters

The three-week decision window for life-altering relocation choices seems particularly aggressive, and the framing as 'natural evolution' strains credibility given the political context of tightening visa enforcement.
