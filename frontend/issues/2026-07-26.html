Librarians are hosting viral ‘Avoiding AI’ workshops for people who are fed up with Big Tech

Librarians across the country are hosting 'Avoiding AI' workshops that have gone viral, teaching people how to disable AI features like Apple Intelligence and Gemini on their devices. Started by Hannah Cyrus, a librarian in Maine, and replicated by Charlie Bailey in Philadelphia and dozens of others, these workshops cover how consumer AI tools work and provide step-by-step instructions for turning them off. The workshops have seen unprecedented demand, with registration filling up quickly, waitlists forming, and Zoom livestreams added to accommodate interest. Bailey framed the effort as advancing digital literacy and helping people reclaim autonomy over whether they use AI tools, noting widespread frustration with AI being forced into everyday technology.

One fallen power line exposed a growing AI data center problem. Here’s how to fix it.

A fallen power line near Washington, DC this week caused over 3 gigawatts of data centers in Northern Virginia to simultaneously disconnect from the PJM grid, triggering voltage spikes from Virginia to Chicago. The incident, which took over 10 minutes to stabilize, highlighted how concentrated AI data center loads can destabilize the electrical grid when they all switch to backup power at once. Experts warn these events will become more frequent as data center capacity grows. Solutions proposed include sequencing data center disconnections/reconnections rather than having them all react simultaneously, and building data centers that can absorb grid disruptions rather than immediately disconnecting.
