TechCrunch AI

Midterm results deal blow to data centers and Elon Musk's political spending

The 2026 U.S. midterm elections delivered a historic Democratic victory, with voters across the country passing ballot measures restricting data center construction. Texas elected governor Gina Hinojosa, who campaigned on restricting data centers, defeating incumbent Greg Abbott. Elon Musk donated over half a billion dollars to Republicans with little return, and his companies are now headquartered in a state that turned blue. Data center opposition has become a bipartisan grassroots issue.

Why it matters

Data center opposition has calcified into a genuine grassroots political movement, and Musk's massive political spending looks like one of the worst return-on-investment stories in recent political history.

More details emerge on China's alleged recruitment of exec to spy on Nvidia CEO

A U.S. intelligence memo claims Alice Huang of Singapore-based Megaspeed was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The Financial Times reported the memo alleges a romantic relationship between the two. Nvidia said Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were 'not unusual' and the company does not share classified information with customers. Separately, the NYT found that New Matrix, a Chinese company authorized to buy Nvidia chips, appears to be a shell with ties to a blacklisted Chinese private equity firm.

The Verge AI

US allowed Nvidia to sell advanced AI chips to apparent Chinese shell company

The New York Times reports that New Matrix, a firm approved to buy Nvidia's H200 AI chips, appears to be a shell company with empty offices, no online presence, and ties to a Chinese private equity firm blacklisted by the US government since 2024. The sales became possible after the Trump administration relaxed Biden-era chip restrictions following Nvidia's agreement to give the government a 15 percent cut of all sales, alongside intense lobbying by CEO Jensen Huang.

Why it matters

A damning indictment of how Nvidia's lobbying and the Trump administration's relaxed export controls may have enabled exactly the technology transfer the restrictions were designed to prevent.

Guardian AI

The case against relentless optimization: why efficiency makes systems fragile

Using examples from hospital bed occupancy to financial markets and transportation, the article argues that running systems at maximum capacity leaves no buffer for crises. Slack, redundancy, and apparent 'waste' are essential for resilience, and our obsession with eliminating inefficiency creates systemic vulnerabilities.

Why it matters

A timely philosophical counterpoint to the Silicon Valley efficiency gospel, particularly relevant as AI promises to optimize everything further.

The Verge AI

Former Meta security chief says AI has broken cybersecurity

Alex Stamos, chief security officer at Cognition and Stanford lecturer, argues on The Verge's Decoder podcast that AI-boosted attacks have created a 'dark age' in cybersecurity. He says the multibillion-dollar cybersecurity industry is failing against AI-enhanced attacks from private and state actors like Russia and North Korea, though he believes this is a difficult transitional period rather than a permanent state.

Why it matters

A sobering assessment from a highly credible voice that the cybersecurity industry's current tools and approaches are fundamentally inadequate for the AI era.
