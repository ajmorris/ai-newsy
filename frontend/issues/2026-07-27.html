What If We Got AI Right? by Eleanor Drage review – avoiding apocalypse

The Guardian reviews Eleanor Drage's book 'What If We Got AI Right?', which attempts to provide an ethical guide to living alongside artificial intelligence. Drage, an AI ethicist, argues that humans need to recognize AI's 'humanity' — acknowledging it as a product of human labor in mines, factories, and data centers rather than treating it as something mystical. The reviewer finds the book ambitious but ultimately falling short of delivering the meaningful answers it promises about AI ethics and power dynamics between citizens and tech companies.

Why it matters

The reviewer appears critical of the book, as indicated by the subtitle 'falls short of its promise to provide meaningful answers.' While acknowledging the ambition of Drage's project and the validity of her core argument about recognizing the human labor behind AI, the review suggests the book does not fully deliver on its goals of providing a comprehensive ethical framework for navigating AI's role in society.

Marketing and engineering tasks travel the farthest, frequently appearing in messages from workers outside those fields. The research also notes more task crossover occurs in small businesses.
