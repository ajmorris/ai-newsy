Amazon doesn’t trust Meta’s Muse AI agent

Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse AI agent from shopping on its platform, citing violations of its Conditions of Use. A popup message now appears for Muse users warning that access by unauthorized AI agents is not permitted. Amazon says Meta never notified them that Muse would access its store and raised privacy and security concerns, including that Muse doesn't identify itself as an AI agent when browsing and appears to capture customer credentials. Meta had claimed at Muse's launch that the agent cannot see secure login details or payment information, but reports have emerged that it can see user message contents without proper permissions. Amazon called on third-party AI shopping agents to operate openly and respect service providers' decisions about participation. This follows Amazon's lawsuit against Perplexity last November over similar agentic shopping concerns.

Why it matters

Amazon's response seems reasonable here. If Meta is sending an AI agent to browse and make purchases on Amazon's platform without identifying itself or coordinating with Amazon, that raises legitimate security and trust concerns — especially around credential handling. The broader tension is significant: as AI agents increasingly act on behalf of users across platforms, there's no established protocol for how they should authenticate, identify themselves, or respect platform boundaries.
