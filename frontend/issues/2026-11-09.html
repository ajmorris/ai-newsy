Anti-AI populism stealing headlines from the algorithms themselves — that's the story this Monday. From voters rejecting datacenter projects across nine states to Google strong-arming schools into accepting its AI tools, today's roundup captures a growing tension between the AI industry's ambitions and the public's appetite for pushing back. We've also got Anthropic dropping $3.4 billion on gas turbines, Trump weighing in on AI safety, and a founder running his company with Lord of the Rings-themed AI agents — because of course someone is.

Business, Deals & Funding

Aeon

Tracing the contested history of 'equality talk' from Euclid to modern politics

Teresa M Bejan traces how the language of equality moved from mathematics to political philosophy, arguing that its apparent simplicity masks deep disagreements about what equality means, and that understanding its historical deployment can clarify modern debates.

Why it matters

A thoughtful reminder that foundational political concepts are far less settled than they appear — useful for anyone frustrated by how 'equality' can mean opposite things to different people.

Guardian AI

Google warns schools: block our AI tools and you may lose Google Search too

Google announced new AI tools for schools but warned that schools opting to block its AI features could also lose access to Google Search. Parents and teachers across the US are calling the move extortion, arguing it removes meaningful choice from educators.

Why it matters

A heavy-handed move that risks a serious backlash — tying a near-essential utility to acceptance of controversial new tools is the kind of bundling that invites regulatory scrutiny.

Harvard Business Review

Overly strict AI policies may backfire by increasing employee resistance

Harvard Business Review argues that rigid AI oversight can increase employee resistance to adoption, and that leaders should instead build trust, invest in skill development, and grant more autonomy in how workers use AI tools.

Why it matters

Sensible but unsurprising advice — the real challenge is calibrating oversight when the risks of unsupervised AI use are still poorly understood.

Lenny's Newsletter

Founder built a Duolingo alternative with Codex and runs his company with Lord of the Rings-themed AI agents

William Imoh built Mural, an AI language tutor, using Codex and GPT-Live after finding that a 1,000-day Duolingo streak didn't help him converse in Norwegian. He also runs his 25-person company with Grok Bots named after Lord of the Rings characters connected to tools like Linear, Notion, and Pipedrive.

Why it matters

An entertaining case study, though the $3-$5 cost per 30-minute voice session highlights that real-time AI tutoring is still expensive compared to traditional apps.

TechCrunch AI

Anthropic spends $3.4B on natural gas turbines to power AI infrastructure

Anthropic is purchasing Caterpillar natural gas turbines capable of producing up to 1.7 gigawatts, acquired after equipment supplier OnePWR failed to secure financing. The move is unusual for Anthropic, which has historically preferred renting infrastructure. Analysts warn natural gas prices could more than triple by decade's end.

Why it matters

A remarkable shift from Anthropic's asset-light strategy that underscores just how desperate the power situation is for AI companies — though locking in natural gas when prices may spike is a significant gamble.

The Verge AI

Trump downplays AI safety risks in 60 Minutes interview, reveals DOJ threatened an AI exec with jail

In a 60 Minutes interview, President Trump said AI safety risks are 'not nearly as real' as executives warn and revealed the DOJ threatened an unnamed AI company leader with jail time for allegedly 'doing something bad with AI.' He said he would work with Democrats on AI guardrails but opposes regulations that put the US behind China, and continued promoting 'super intelligence' over 'artificial intelligence' as terminology.

Why it matters

The revelation that DOJ threatened an AI executive with jail is the buried lede here — it suggests enforcement actions are already happening behind the scenes, even as the president publicly dismisses safety concerns.

Guardian AI

Anti-AI populism emerges as a defining force in American politics after 2026 midterms

Garrison Lovely argues in The Guardian that anti-AI populism is a major new political force following the Democrats' 2026 midterm sweep. Voters rejected datacenter projects in over 30 localities across nine states, 23% of voters cited AI as crucial to their decision, and AI industry leaders have effectively cast themselves as villains through their own statements about job destruction and existential risk. Lovely predicts AI will be the defining fight of the 2028 presidential cycle.

Update

Why it matters

A compelling argument that AI leaders' own doom-and-disruption rhetoric has handed populists the narrative on a platter — the 2028 implications are worth watching closely.

TechCrunch AI

Medium launches TK, a writing app with built-in AI assistance

Medium launched TK, a writing app where users invoke AI by typing '@TK' for quick questions, notes retrieval, and editorial feedback matched to publication style guides. Addressing controversy around AI in publishing, Medium positions AI as amplifying human writing and offers transparency features letting authors explain how AI was used.

Why it matters

A pragmatic middle ground in the AI-writing debate — the transparency mechanism is a smart move, though whether readers will actually check provenance notes remains to be seen.

The Verge AI

Disney and Adobe partner to build proprietary generative AI models for Disney properties

Disney and Adobe announced a multiyear partnership to develop 'artist-led' generative AI models trained on Disney's characters, environments, and visual styles. Announced at Adobe's MAX conference, the deal gives Disney early access to Adobe Firefly Foundry for use across theme parks, cruise lines, ESPN content, and production pipelines.

Why it matters

A significant legitimization of generative AI in entertainment — Disney's involvement signals that major IP holders see custom AI models as a way to control rather than resist the technology.

TechCrunch AI

Automattic launches Spacefast, a platform for deploying vibe-coded apps

Automattic launched Spacefast, a platform for deploying vibe-coded websites and apps that provides infrastructure (databases, authentication, file uploads, analytics) while letting users choose their own AI coding agent. Created during a company hackathon, it differentiates from competitors like Lovable by leveraging Automattic's existing CDN, WordPress integration, and WooCommerce capabilities.

Why it matters

A shrewd infrastructure play — rather than competing on AI coding itself, Automattic bets that the deployment and hosting layer is where lasting value accrues as vibe-coding tools commoditize.

The Verge AI

RIAA CEO discusses the music industry's fight against AI companies over copyright

In a Decoder podcast interview, RIAA CEO Mitch Glazier discussed how the music industry, artists, and ordinary people now find themselves aligned against AI companies over unauthorized training on copyrighted works. Topics included AI deepfake legal complexities, potential bans and their free expression implications, and challenges of AI detection if platforms like Spotify and YouTube don't cooperate.

Why it matters

The full-circle irony of the RIAA — once the villain suing Napster users — now positioned as defender of creators against Big Tech makes for a genuinely interesting narrative shift.
