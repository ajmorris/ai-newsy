Nvidia in crisis after Jensen Huang admits affair with suspected Chinese spy

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, whom US intelligence agencies suspect of spying for China. Nvidia's stock dropped roughly 9.5% over two days. The board has hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an internal investigation. Key questions center on what information Alice Huang may have obtained and when Jensen Huang became aware of espionage suspicions. Experts warn that failing to inform the board could be a fireable offense, and shareholder lawsuits including securities fraud claims are expected. The incident threatens US AI leadership given Nvidia's critical role as the dominant designer of advanced AI chips, with major companies like Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI dependent on its products. The board has stated it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.

DeepMind Blog

Google DeepMind makes Gemini 4 Carbon generally available

Google DeepMind announced the general availability of Gemini 4 Carbon on November 10, 2026. Carbon is positioned as their strongest frontier model, with leading performance in cybersecurity, long-horizon agentic workflows, complex knowledge work, software engineering, and scientific reasoning. It was initially released to cybersecurity defenders via Google's Fairwind Program two weeks prior. Carbon complements the earlier Gemini 4 Argon model — Argon is recommended for most software engineering and knowledge work, while Carbon targets tasks at the limits of model intelligence such as complex multi-step coding/debugging and open-ended scientific research.

Why it matters

The cybersecurity-first rollout via the Fairwind Program was a smart trust-building move. The Argon/Carbon split mirrors Anthropic's Sonnet/Opus tiering — the frontier AI market is converging on a 'good enough for most tasks' plus 'maximum capability' product structure.

Guardian AI

AI datacenter developers planning 181GW of on-site gas-fired power stations

Research reveals that AI datacenter developers across the US, UK, and mainland Europe are planning to build on-site gas-fired power stations totaling 181GW of capacity. This massive buildout of fossil fuel infrastructure is being driven by delays in connecting datacenters to national electricity grids, as the rapid expansion of AI computing demands more power than existing grid connections can supply.

Why it matters

181GW is a staggering figure — roughly equivalent to the entire installed electricity generation capacity of the UK. This undermines the clean energy commitments many tech companies have made and suggests the AI boom's environmental costs are being systematically underestimated.

TechCrunch AI

Plurai raises $13M to improve AI agent reliability using autonomous driving simulation techniques

Plurai, founded by former Nexar employees who worked on autonomous driving technology, raised $13 million in seed funding to improve AI agent reliability. The company creates digital twins of corporate electronic systems, maps all possible agent workflows, and uses synthetic data to evaluate and fix edge cases. Their key value proposition is post-training smaller, open-weight models tailored to individual customers, claiming to match or exceed frontier model quality at one to two orders of magnitude lower cost. The round was led by Team8, with participation from U&I, Velocity, and Mercer.

Why it matters

The autonomous driving simulation analogy is compelling — systematically testing edge cases before deployment is exactly what enterprise AI agents need. The claim of matching frontier model quality at 1-2 orders of magnitude lower cost deserves scrutiny but would be transformative if true.

OpenAI acquires AI travel startup Odessia in apparent acqui-hire

OpenAI acquired Odessia, a year-old AI travel agent startup founded by Francis Davidson, the well-known founder of Sonder. The deal appears to be an acqui-hire, with Davidson now working at OpenAI on travel-related initiatives. Odessia had raised a $6 million pre-seed round led by Sequoia and helped users plan millions of dollars worth of travel before being shut down. Travel is a frequent use case among ChatGPT's 1.2 billion weekly users, and OpenAI already partners with Booking.com, Expedia, and KAYAK.

Why it matters

With 1.2 billion weekly users frequently asking about travel, building native travel capabilities makes obvious commercial sense. The acqui-hire of a Sequoia-backed founder signals OpenAI is serious about vertical commerce, not just horizontal AI.

Guardian AI

Guardian podcast revisits ELIZA creator Joseph Weizenbaum's turn against AI

A Guardian podcast revisits a 2023 article about Joseph Weizenbaum, the MIT computer scientist who created ELIZA in 1966. Weizenbaum was disturbed when users formed genuine emotional attachments to the program despite knowing it was just software. This experience, combined with observations of how computers were being applied to military and surveillance systems during the Cold War, led him to become one of AI's most prominent internal critics. His 1976 book 'Computer Power and Human Reason' argued that there are tasks computers should never be entrusted with — not because they can't perform them, but because doing so undermines human judgment and responsibility.

Why it matters

Weizenbaum's core argument — that capability is not the same as appropriateness — feels more relevant than ever in the age of frontier AI models. A timely archival piece given current debates about AI agent autonomy and emotional attachment to chatbots.
