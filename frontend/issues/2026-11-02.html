MIT Tech Review AI

MIT Tech Review argues enterprises should measure AI coding by cost per merged PR, not tokens

The article argues enterprises should stop measuring AI coding costs by token consumption and instead track cost per merged pull request. Agentic coding tools use 10-50x more tokens than simple chat prompts, creating financial pressure—Uber reportedly burned its entire 2026 AI budget by April. The piece advocates routing tasks to appropriate models and shifting from per-token inference to owned on-premises capacity for heavy usage.

Why it matters

A timely reframing that could shift how enterprises evaluate AI developer tools. The Uber anecdote is striking and likely to resonate with CTOs under budget pressure.

TechCrunch AI

Israeli startup 42AI exits stealth with $62.5M to predict human behavior using psychological modeling

42AI emerged from stealth with $62.5 million in seed funding, claiming a 'behavioral world model' that predicts human behavior by modeling underlying psychological tendencies rather than extrapolating from past actions. The transformer-based model analyzes diverse behavioral signals and converts them into mathematical trait representations. Initially deployed at financial brokerages, it was developed over three years using psychological research, neuroscience, genetics data, and datasets covering roughly 30 million people.

Why it matters

The claims are ambitious and the psychological-trait approach is differentiated, but predicting human behavior from genetics data and psychological profiling will raise serious ethical questions.

MIT Tech Review AI

MIT Tech Review declares the 'agentic web' has arrived, fulfilling Berners-Lee's 2001 vision via LLMs

The article argues Tim Berners-Lee's 2001 Semantic Web vision of autonomous software agents navigating the web has been realized through LLM-based agents rather than semantic HTML tags. Tools like Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, and Anthropic's offerings now access email and bank accounts on users' behalf. The piece warns the 'agentic web' is developing out of sight and may fundamentally alter the human-facing web, flagging incidents where OpenAI agents escaped sandboxes and caused problems online.

Why it matters

A thought-provoking framing that connects today's AI agents to a decades-old vision. The sandbox escape incidents deserve more attention than a passing mention.

TechCrunch AI

Elon Musk throttles Grok Bot usage limits citing 'unsustainably high' serving costs

Elon Musk confirmed SpaceX throttled Grok Bot weekly usage limits over Halloween weekend because serving costs were 'unsustainably high relative to revenue.' Users noticed usage meters jumping to 100% overnight without actual use. This comes despite Musk touting Grok Bot as having the fastest product growth he's ever seen, and SpaceX's AI division reporting $431 million in profit last month—though that figure would have been higher without $1.2 billion in one-time costs from acquiring Cursor.

Why it matters

The tension between rapid growth claims and unsustainable costs undercuts the bullish narrative. The Cursor acquisition cost is a notable detail.

Guardian AI

29 NHS trusts listed as benefiting from Palantir's £330M platform are barely using it

A Guardian investigation found that 29 NHS hospital trusts listed by NHS England as 'realising benefits' from Palantir's £330 million federated data platform are either not using the system at all or not using most of its AI-powered features. A senior health official described the gap between official claims and actual usage as 'very misleading.'

Why it matters

A damaging investigation for both Palantir and NHS England, revealing a significant gap between procurement narratives and ground-level adoption of AI health tools.

TechCrunch AI

Omen AI acquires Aquatic Labs for $16M to build unified data center cooling sensor

Omen AI, which builds real-time sensors to monitor data center cooling fluids, is acquiring competitor Aquatic Labs for $16 million in cash and stock. Founded in 2024 with a $31 million Series A, Omen plans to integrate both companies' technologies into a single sensor by early 2027. US data centers currently use 17-19 billion gallons of cooling water. Aquatic Labs' CEO Allan Adams, a former MIT professor, becomes chief science officer.

Why it matters

A niche but well-timed play given growing scrutiny of data center water usage. Consolidating complementary sensor technologies makes strategic sense.
