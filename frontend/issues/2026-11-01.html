Apple CEO Ternus says the company doesn't 'want to ship AI for AI's sake'

Apple CEO John Ternus, who took the role in September 2026, defended Apple's AI positioning in a Wired interview. He acknowledged criticism over being behind on AI, particularly after delaying a more powerful Siri that launched in summer 2026, but said he likes where Apple is and emphasized that users shouldn't have to think about whether AI or traditional software powers a feature. When asked about AI agents making apps unnecessary, Ternus called product creation 'a deeply human and personal thing.'

Why it matters

The messaging is classic Apple restraint, but after years of playing catch-up on AI, the 'we don't ship for AI's sake' line will only hold if Apple Intelligence delivers meaningfully differentiated user experiences soon.

Verge writer builds $50 DIY AI Tamagotchi before Meta's Muse Charm ships

Sean Hollister of The Verge built a DIY AI Tamagotchi pendant for $50 using a Waveshare ESP32-S3 touchscreen devkit, connecting it to Meta's Muse AI cloud with open-source code. The device features push-to-talk AI interaction, cute avatars, and doubles as a pocket watch and remote camera viewfinder. While it lacks the Muse Charm's cameras and 5G radio, Hollister found the build required significant 'vibe coding' to add features like smart home control.

Why it matters

A fun demonstration of how accessible AI hardware tinkering has become, though it also highlights that Meta's upcoming Muse Charm will need to offer substantially more polish to justify a premium over hobbyist alternatives.

TechCrunch AI

Judge allows NAACP lawsuit over xAI Colossus data center pollution to proceed

A federal judge denied xAI's motion to dismiss an NAACP lawsuit alleging the company operated gas turbines at its Colossus data center near Memphis without required Clean Air Act permits. Judge Debra M. Brown rejected xAI's arguments that the turbines qualify as exempt mobile sources. The NAACP claims xAI installed at least 35 combustion turbines without preconstruction or operating air permits. The ruling comes amid growing backlash against data centers, including a New York moratorium on new facilities.

Why it matters

A significant legal precedent that could force AI infrastructure companies to comply with existing environmental regulations rather than claiming exemptions, and adds to mounting political pressure on data center expansion.
