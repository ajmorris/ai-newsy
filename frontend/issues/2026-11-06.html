Protesters break into Microsoft data center construction site in Amsterdam

Around 100 protesters from the group Geef Tegengas broke into a Microsoft data center construction site in Amsterdam on November 6, scaling a crane to hang a 'fuck AI' banner and displaying a 'Microsoft powers genocide' banner with 'free Palestine' chants. The project involves three 85-meter towers proceeding despite Amsterdam's ban on new data centers until 2030, as it predates the ban. Update

Why it matters

This escalation from planned protests to physical site break-ins signals that the European anti-data-center movement is intensifying beyond symbolic demonstrations. The regulatory loophole angle—project approved before the ban—will fuel further backlash.

METR finds Anthropic's AI progress running 2x faster, with AI driving 80% of speedup

AI-evaluation nonprofit METR published findings from 11 weeks of inside access to Anthropic, estimating the company's AI progress is running roughly 2x faster than its 2024-25 pace, with AI itself responsible for about 80% of the acceleration. METR found no clear bottlenecks to slow this trend. The report revealed two internal models ahead of publicly available Claude Opus 5.5, including a 'Model 4' that cleared 50% on all 15 tested benchmarks. Anthropic heavily redacted the strongest evidence. Anthropic's own IPO filing defined a danger threshold as 'dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI,' and METR's findings suggest that point may be approaching.

Why it matters

This is the most consequential AI governance disclosure in months. An independent evaluator confirming recursive self-improvement at 2x pace—with AI doing most of the work—puts Anthropic's own stated danger threshold in play. The heavy redactions suggest the full picture is even more striking.

Ars Technica AI

Decade-old 'sshole' vulnerability lets attackers SSH into Linux devices without a password

A decade-old vulnerability nicknamed 'sshole' allows attackers to log into Linux devices via SSH without any password by entering more than 256 characters in the password field. It affects systems running OpenSSH with musl libc without PAM, most notably Alpine Linux. The root cause is disputed between OpenSSH and musl developers, though both have issued patches. Impact is potentially large given Alpine's widespread use in containers (over 1 billion Docker Hub downloads), embedded devices, routers, and IoT, many of which are difficult to update.

Why it matters

The simplicity of exploitation—just type a long password—combined with Alpine's massive container footprint makes this a high-urgency patching event. The finger-pointing between OpenSSH and musl is concerning because it suggests the bug class may recur.

MIT Tech Review AI

Tau Robotics sends remotely piloted humanoid robots to clean San Francisco homes for $60/week

MIT Technology Review tested Tau Robotics' new humanoid-robot cleaning service in San Francisco. The startup sends remotely piloted four-and-a-half-foot-tall humanoid robots to clean homes for $60/week or $100 for a one-time visit. The robots have 3D-printed claw hands and wear grippy socks. While physically capable, they are slow and require significant human guidance—many tasks use kinematic guidance where a human pilot's movements are mirrored. Some tasks like vacuuming can be done autonomously. The real business model is data collection: every session generates training data to eventually build fully autonomous cleaning robots.

Why it matters

This is a clever data flywheel disguised as a cleaning service—customers pay to generate the training data that will eventually replace the human pilots. The $60/week price point is competitive enough with human cleaners to sustain the model while the autonomy improves.

Startup Underdog demos 32MB text-to-speech model that runs entirely in the browser

AI startup Underdog demoed Woof, a 32MB (31 million parameters) text-to-speech model that runs entirely in the browser without internet. The company showcased it through an interactive retro-themed game. The open model follows Underdog's recent Bark release, a 167MB speech-to-text model also for edge devices. TechCrunch AI

Physicl raises $10M to build high-fidelity 3D environments for robot training

Physicl, founded by Alex de Vigan (previously of nfinite), raised $10M in seed funding from Inovia Capital to repurpose e-commerce 3D rendering technology for robotics training. The company addresses the sim-to-real gap by transforming real-world information into 3D physical representations with correct geometry, materials, and physics. Their Studio product offers 10,000 3D environments and 100,000 object assets for generating synthetic training data, focusing on difficult edge cases.

Why it matters

The e-commerce-to-robotics pivot is smart—3D rendering for product visualization and 3D rendering for robot training share core technical requirements but the robotics market has far higher growth potential.
