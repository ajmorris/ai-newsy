Our approach to EU text provenance rules

OpenAI announced its approach to complying with the EU AI Act's text provenance requirements. Their technology, called textGrain, embeds an invisible statistical watermark into model-generated text. Starting now, API customers can opt in globally, and ChatGPT/Codex output in the EU will be watermarked over coming weeks. Detection access is initially limited to approved researchers. Performance varies: at a 1% false positive rate, detection hits ~80% for 200-token passages and ~95% for 400-token passages in flexible domains like psychology, but drops significantly for constrained domains like math. Light editing (10% synonym replacement) cuts detection from ~92% to ~66%, and heavier editing (25%) drops it to ~17%. OpenAI plans to open-source the technology and notes that their existing image and audio provenance tools remain publicly available.

Why it matters

This is a measured and relatively transparent rollout. OpenAI deserves credit for publishing concrete detection rates, acknowledging the brittleness of text watermarking under editing, and limiting detector access to researchers rather than releasing a half-baked public tool that could be misused for false accusations of AI authorship.
