Anthropic completes largest-ever IPO, shares whipsaw in first two days of trading

Anthropic listed on Nasdaq under ticker ANT at $83/share, raising $81.3 billion and surpassing SpaceX's record from five months prior. Shares opened at $111.50 and closed day one at $97.17 (up 17%), briefly valuing the company near $2 trillion. 96% of Anthropic's 5,000+ employees became paper millionaires, while early investors Amazon and Google saw their stakes reach $161 billion and $147 billion respectively. CEO Dario Amodei pledged that safety, not business or political pressure, would guide development pace. On day two, shares fell over 9% to $88.17 as a broader AI sector sell-off gripped global markets, with Asian markets recording their worst week since 2020 amid investor concerns about massive AI infrastructure spending.

Ars Technica AI

Russian national indicted for spying on Silicon Valley defense company employees on behalf of Moscow

Igor D'iu, a 25-year-old Russian national, has been indicted for allegedly spying on employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defense company (reported by Reuters and Bloomberg to be Palantir) on behalf of Russia. D'iu allegedly entered the US unlawfully in summer 2026 after being recruited via Telegram, receiving instructions and funding to surveil company employees because the firm supplied weapons-related technology for use against Russian forces in Ukraine. His activities included photographing employees and their computer screens at a coffee shop near the company's Palo Alto office, compiling home addresses, and aggressively tailing a female employee for nearly 30 miles — an incident that ended only when she flagged a police patrol car.

Why it matters

The case illustrates how traditional physical espionage is being directed at AI and defense tech companies, blending old-school surveillance tradecraft with the geopolitics of the Ukraine conflict.

Pew Research Center

McClatchy pivots to AI-native newsroom as Pew data shows majority of Americans distrust AI in news

McClatchy, parent of the Miami Herald and Kansas City Star, is shifting to an 'AI-native operating model' after declaring its current business model unsustainable due to declining web traffic and subscribers. The move follows earlier staff cuts and has caused disputes with reporters. Pew Research data shows 56% of Americans say AI in news articles would decrease their confidence, Americans are five times more likely to predict negative than positive AI impact on news over 20 years, and 59% expect fewer journalism jobs.

Why it matters

McClatchy's AI bet is a gamble against its own audience's stated preferences — most Americans say AI in news would make them trust it less, which makes the sustainability argument circular.

Guardian AI

AI adds hidden cognitive burden on lowest-paid workers, analysis argues

An analysis argues AI is imposing a hidden 'cognitive tax' on the lowest-paid workers, adding to the cognitive load of those already stretched thin by unpredictable pay, insecure housing, and mounting debt. The piece calls for rethinking welfare systems for an AI-reshaped economy, noting that throughout economics history, the human brain — which directs labor, judgment, and resilience — has rarely been considered an asset worth protecting.

Why it matters

Framing cognitive load as a regressive tax on workers least equipped to absorb it offers a useful policy lens for discussions that tend to focus narrowly on job displacement rather than the degradation of existing jobs.

Pew Research Center

Pew survey finds broad satisfaction with 2026 midterm election administration

A Pew Research Center survey of 10,247 U.S. adults finds 83% of voters say elections across the country were run well, with 94% saying the same about their own community. Voters express more confidence in in-person than mail-in voting. The survey is part of a larger report on postelection attitudes following the Democrats' midterm victory, which found majority approval of the party's policies and plans.

Why it matters

The high satisfaction numbers, especially at the local level, suggest election administration concerns are less salient to voters than political rhetoric implies.

The Verge AI

Signal blocks Gemini and Siri AI from reading users' screens by default

Signal is restricting screenshot capabilities on Android and iOS to prevent AI agents like Gemini and Siri from reading users' messages and on-screen data. Signal president Meredith Whittaker criticized OS-level agentic AI features as 'hostile to privacy' and urged Google, Apple, and Microsoft to provide better tools for app developers to control AI data access. Users can re-enable screenshots in Signal's settings, but the default will block them. Signal noted it cannot implement these protections on macOS due to insufficient developer controls from Apple.

Why it matters

Signal's move highlights an emerging battleground between messaging privacy and OS-level AI agents — and the macOS gap suggests Apple's privacy reputation may not extend to giving developers the tools to enforce it.
