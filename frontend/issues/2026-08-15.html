Vulnerability giving attackers full control of Macs is under active exploitation

A high-severity macOS vulnerability (CVE-2026-65400) in the screen sharing feature is being actively exploited in the wild. The bug, rated 7.1/10, allows remote attackers without credentials to gain root access to Macs when port 5900 is exposed to the Internet. Dutch cybersecurity officials (NCSC) reported multiple cases where attackers gained root access and installed Monero cryptocurrency miners. The vulnerability stems from a state management flaw in macOS screen sharing and was patched by Apple last week for macOS Tahoe, Sequoia, and Sonoma. Details were disclosed at Black Hat security conference. Security experts recommend keeping port 5900 closed, disabling screen sharing when not in use, using VPN or SSH tunneling instead of direct exposure, and installing the latest security update.

This is a serious vulnerability that highlights a recurring problem: features designed for convenience (like screen sharing) becoming attack vectors when left enabled and exposed to the Internet. The fact that it allows unauthenticated root access remotely is particularly alarming — a 7.1 severity rating almost feels understated for a no-credentials-needed remote root exploit. Science Daily

World’s first superconducting quantum heat engine could help unlock massive quantum computers

Researchers at Aalto University have built the world's first cyclic superconducting quantum heat engine, published in Nature Communications. The device combines a transmon qubit, a resonator, and a quantum circuit refrigerator, operating near absolute zero to convert quantum-scale heat into useful work by reproducing an Otto cycle within a superconducting circuit. The quantum circuit refrigerator can be tuned to provide both heating and cooling, eliminating the need for separate hot and cold environments. This proof of concept demonstrates that familiar thermodynamic processes can function under quantum conditions, and future autonomous versions could potentially be integrated inside quantum computers to eliminate large numbers of costly, noise-producing microwave cables, helping enable the scaling of quantum computers to very large numbers of qubits.

This is a genuinely exciting and elegant piece of research that bridges two fundamental domains of physics — quantum mechanics and thermodynamics — in a practical experimental demonstration.
