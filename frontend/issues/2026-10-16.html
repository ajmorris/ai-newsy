Meta launches Muse Spark Open, an 816B open-weight model that runs locally on Macs

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight multimodal AI model with a 1-million-token context window, developed in partnership with Apple to run locally on Apple silicon Macs using the MLX toolkit. The model is built for complex coding and agentic tasks but requires at least 1TB of RAM, running best on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios. Meta claims it is the most capable open-weight model in the US. The release signals Meta's return to open-weight AI after shifting toward closed models with its frontier Muse Wonder.

Ars Technica AI

Mercenary hackers used targeted advertising to deliver zero-click spyware on iOS and Android

Citizen Lab researchers discovered a campaign dubbed AdFection in which Israeli company Discover-Tech brokered access between ad networks and mercenary spyware operators to deliver zero-click exploits via targeted ads. At least 45 devices at Al Jazeera were infected in 2024-2025, requiring no user interaction. Discover-Tech's ads appeared in over 70,000 mobile apps and 120,000 websites, reaching NATO, UN, and European government networks. Google, IAB Europe, and Beeswax have since removed Discover-Tech from their platforms.

Why it matters

This reveals a deeply alarming attack surface: the programmatic ad ecosystem, already opaque and difficult to police, being weaponized for state-level surveillance. The sheer reach — 70,000 apps, 120,000 websites — suggests the actual infection count may far exceed what Citizen Lab has documented.

OpenAI

OpenAI offers free GPT-Rosalind access to academic research labs

OpenAI announced the Rosalind Academic Plan, offering eligible academic research labs up to 30 Business Standard seats at no cost for up to 12 months with access to GPT-Rosalind, a specialized life sciences AI model. GPT-Rosalind combines frontier reasoning with a 'Rosalind workbench' for tool orchestration across medicinal chemistry, genomics, wet-lab assistance, and other scientific applications. Labs at UCSF and elsewhere are already using it for drug parameter gathering, literature exploration, experiment refinement, and protein design workflows.

Why it matters

A smart strategic move — seeding academic labs with free access builds both goodwill and dependency. The specialized workbench approach for life sciences is more compelling than general-purpose models for this audience, though the 12-month limit raises questions about what happens to ongoing research when the free period ends.

Pew Research Center

Turkish police raid critical TV news channel Halk TV, halting broadcasts

Turkish police raided the headquarters of Halk TV, one of the few Turkish news channels critical of President Erdoğan, halting live broadcasts and appointing a government administrator. The government cites financial crimes involving the network's chairman, but critics view it as suppression of dissent. A 2024 Pew Research Center survey found 91% of Turkish adults believe media should report without government censorship, yet about half said Turkish media are not very free.

Why it matters

The Halk TV raid follows a well-established playbook of using financial allegations to justify crackdowns on critical media. The stark contrast between public support for press freedom and the reality of government control underscores the erosion of democratic norms.

TechCrunch AI

Cal AI hit $30M revenue in two years through aggressive influencer marketing

Cal AI, an AI calorie tracking app founded by 19-year-old Zach Yadegari in 2024, achieved over $30 million in revenue in two years through a large-scale influencer marketing strategy using college students paid on performance-based rates. The app was acquired by MyFitnessPal in March 2026. Yadegari shared these details at TechCrunch Disrupt and announced his new venture Persona, an AI agent startup that has raised $10 million to build a personal AI assistant across multiple platforms.

Why it matters

Cal AI is a compelling case study in growth hacking rather than AI innovation — the real story is the influencer playbook, not the technology. Yadegari's pivot to an AI agent startup with Persona suggests he sees the calorie app as a stepping stone rather than a destination.

The Verge AI

ChatGPT Chrome extension can now read and interact with open browser tabs

OpenAI updated its ChatGPT Chrome extension to allow ChatGPT to view and interact with users' open browser tabs. Users can tag Chrome in the web or desktop app to have the AI read tab contents for tasks like product comparison, form filling, and email drafting. For ChatGPT Work users, the extension can actively control Chrome tabs to send messages and download files. The update comes after OpenAI discontinued its standalone ChatGPT Atlas browser.

Why it matters

This is a significant expansion of ChatGPT's surface area into users' daily browsing, and the privacy implications are substantial — reading all open tabs means potential exposure of sensitive financial, medical, or personal data. The work-tier active control features especially blur the line between assistant and autonomous agent.

HR Dive

Employee well-being improves but financial pressures drive workers to skip medical care

A WTW report finds employee well-being has improved since 2019, with 64% of workers saying they are physically thriving — up 10%. However, financial pressures are causing many to delay or skip medical care, with 41% of people in their 40s and 21% of employees 60 and older avoiding doctor appointments due to cost. Experts recommend employers address this through more affordable coverage, co-pay plans with consolidated billing, and assessing risk tolerance for high-cost claims.

Why it matters

The headline improvement in well-being masks a troubling undercurrent: workers in their 40s — typically peak earning years — are skipping care at alarming rates, suggesting cost-shifting in employer plans is creating a false economy that could lead to more expensive downstream health crises.

OpenAI launches AI for Educators course in Brazilian Portuguese on Teachers' Day

OpenAI announced the launch of its AI for Educators course in Brazilian Portuguese on OpenAI Academy to celebrate Teachers' Day. The course covers using AI for lesson planning, creating activities and assessments, and preparing communications. The announcement also highlights Study Mode in ChatGPT, a feature that guides students through subjects using questions, hints, and comprehension checks rather than providing direct answers.

Why it matters

A smart localization play for a massive market — Brazil has over 2.5 million teachers. The Study Mode feature represents the more responsible side of AI in education, though its effectiveness compared to simply asking ChatGPT for answers depends heavily on student willingness to opt into the slower guided learning path.

The Verge AI

Sega pulls Total War: Shogun 2 remaster after backlash over AI-upscaled artwork and bugs

Sega pulled Total War: Shogun 2 — Complete Edition from sale after overwhelmingly negative Steam reviews. The remaster, developed with partner studio Mataboo, promised improved visuals and UI scaling but delivered bugs and artwork that users identified as poorly upscaled using AI. Sega and Creative Assembly acknowledged the game fell short of expectations and committed to reinstating the original game and fixing the issues.

Why it matters

A cautionary tale for the gaming industry's rush to use AI for cost-cutting on remasters. Players can clearly detect low-effort AI upscaling, and the reputational damage to a beloved franchise likely exceeds whatever was saved on art production costs.

Guardian AI

AI surveillance tools expand workplace monitoring beyond traditional 'bossware'

The Guardian reports on the growing use of AI-powered surveillance tools in workplaces that go beyond earlier 'bossware' monitoring software. These AI systems can process large volumes of data to provide detailed, minute-by-minute tracking of worker activity and productivity. The piece features UK technology editor Robert Booth discussing which sectors are adopting AI surveillance, the motivations behind it, and how such monitoring could prove counterproductive.

Why it matters

The evolution from simple screen-capture bossware to AI-driven behavioral analytics represents a qualitative shift in workplace power dynamics. Research consistently shows that heavy monitoring undermines the trust and autonomy that drive creative and knowledge work productivity.

HR Dive

Walmart expands healthcare benefits with free prescriptions, paid NICU leave, and rare disease coverage

Walmart announced expanded healthcare benefits including free prescriptions for over 250 medications (including GLP-1s for type 2 diabetes), 12 weeks of paid NICU leave starting November 17, and a new rare conditions center of excellence providing free specialist access through Mayo Clinic and Cleveland Clinic for conditions like MS, Parkinson's, and ALS. Premiums are increasing $1.50 per pay period to $39.80 on the starting plan, amid broader industry trends of nearly double-digit health insurance cost increases.

Why it matters

The GLP-1 inclusion is the standout detail — while most employers are trying to limit access to these expensive drugs, Walmart is offering them free for diabetes patients. The rare disease center of excellence through Mayo and Cleveland Clinic is genuinely differentiated for a retail employer. The modest premium increase positions Walmart favorably against peers shifting costs more aggressively.

TechCrunch AI

Amazon unveils 'Bee' wearable devices for passive AI-powered ambient computing

Amazon devices chief Panos Panay outlined a vision for ambient AI computing at TechCrunch Disrupt, where devices work passively in the background using sensors and AI to anticipate user needs. Amazon launched new 'Bee' wearable devices — a wrist band/pendant and a magnetic smartphone attachment — that passively record audio, extract to-dos and insights in real time, then delete the raw audio. Transcripts are encrypted with user-controlled keys. Panay predicted a generational shift toward technology that automatically understands needs rather than requiring users to open specific apps.

Why it matters

Amazon is making another bet on ambient computing after Alexa's middling results, but this time with wearables that record everything around you. The privacy design — deleting raw audio and encrypting transcripts — is the right approach, though consumer willingness to wear always-on microphones remains the fundamental barrier, not the technology.

The Verge AI

Hollywood releases wave of films taking aim at Silicon Valley leaders

The Verge reports that 2026 has seen an unusual surge of Hollywood films critically examining Big Tech, including Aaron Sorkin's 'The Social Reckoning' portraying Zuckerberg as destabilizing, Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim's 'You Can See Everything' on Elizabeth Holmes facing prison, Alex Gibney's 'Musk' documentary reflecting shifted public opinion, and Luca Guadagnino's 'Artificial,' a dark comedy about Sam Altman. The article frames this cluster as evidence that the tech backlash has moved from online discourse into mainstream theatrical filmmaking.

Why it matters

Four major productions targeting four different tech figures in a single year is unprecedented and signals that filmmakers see audience appetite for tech-critical narratives. The Altman film is particularly notable given that AI is still in its ascendant phase — usually Hollywood waits for a fall before dramatizing it.
