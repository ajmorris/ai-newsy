Mythos attack on 3rd-round PQC algorithm candidate puts it out of commission

Anthropic's Mythos AI security model helped discover a fatal flaw in HAWK, a post-quantum cryptographic digital signature algorithm that was in its third round of NIST evaluation. The AI-assisted attack effectively cut HAWK's key strength in half by finding previously unknown automorphism symmetries in the Lattice Isomorphism Problem underlying HAWK's security. Following the announcement, HAWK's developer withdrew it from consideration. Mythos also found weaknesses in AES, though the article notes several caveats: the results are incremental, tested against weakened challenge instances rather than production versions, the underlying mathematical primitives remain safe, and the attacks would be infeasible outside testing environments. The work took about 60 hours and $100,000 in compute, conducted by a researcher with no cryptography expertise prompting the AI model.

Why it matters

This is a genuinely significant development that deserves serious attention despite the marketing angle. The fact that an AI model, prompted by a non-expert, found a weakness in a cryptographic algorithm that survived years of expert peer review is remarkable and somewhat alarming.
