The AI industry is swimming in money and drowning in contradictions. OpenAI just raised $11 billion at a staggering $1.25 trillion valuation — even as midterm voters signaled deep skepticism about AI's unchecked expansion — while a new METR report from inside Anthropic reveals AI development is accelerating twice as fast as last year, with the technology increasingly building itself. Today's digest is a snapshot of an industry sprinting forward on every front: Google pouring $17 billion into data centers, data center developers quietly abandoning their green energy promises, China allegedly planting spies near Nvidia's CEO, and a major blockchain firm exposing FBI credentials to hackers.

Ars Technica AI

Chainalysis discloses major breach exposing credentials for all customers including FBI and Microsoft

Blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis disclosed a breach in which hackers stole credentials, password hashes, and live authentication tokens for all customers, including government agencies (FBI, ICE, US Air Force) and companies (Microsoft, Coinbase, Barclays). The company described AI agent-assisted offensive techniques and an on-path attack. Chainalysis took down its login page and is requiring mandatory credential rotation, but key details remain unanswered including the attack source and why disclosure was delayed two days.

Why it matters

The involvement of AI-assisted offensive techniques marks an escalation in real-world cyberattacks, and the high-profile customer list—especially law enforcement and intelligence agencies—makes this breach exceptionally consequential for national security.

TechCrunch AI

Crusoe sells troubled Wyoming data center project to Google for $17 billion investment

Denver-based data center developer Crusoe (valued at $30.9 billion) sold its unfinished Wyoming data center project to Google, which plans to invest $17 billion to develop the campus. Crusoe had originally announced the project with Blackstone-backed Tallgrass with plans to scale to 10 gigawatts. Google had reportedly raised concerns about cost and timeline under Crusoe's management. Crusoe continues to work with Google on a separate Texas project and counts Oracle, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta, and Jane Street among its clients.

Why it matters

Google swooping in on a troubled project it was already scrutinizing suggests the hyperscalers are gaining even more leverage over independent data center developers—Crusoe's $30.9B valuation may not insulate it from being a build-to-sell operation for bigger players.

The Verge AI

METR report on Anthropic's AI-accelerated development draws alarm from researchers

METR conducted an 11-week investigation inside Anthropic and found AI development is progressing roughly twice as fast as a year ago, with AI driving most of the acceleration. Anthropic was allowed to redact portions of the report, raising transparency concerns. The findings come as Anthropic prepares to go public while CEO Dario Amodei has called for labs to slow down, highlighting tensions between commercial ambitions and safety rhetoric.

Guardian AI

Lanarkshire AI data centre abandons renewables pledge, will draw entirely from National Grid

DataVita, a data centre company central to the UK's AI strategy, has admitted its massive 1GW AI development near Glasgow will be entirely powered by the National Grid, reversing its original promise to use renewable energy equivalent to the output of a small nuclear reactor or Britain's largest windfarm.

Why it matters

This reversal exposes a pattern where data centre developers use green energy pledges to win planning approvals, then quietly switch to grid power—undermining both climate commitments and public trust in AI infrastructure projects.

Guardian AI

Southeast Asia's data centre boom hits wall as power and water constraints mount

Southeast Asia's data centre expansion is stalling as power and water constraints create obstacles. Thailand, which had approved $27 billion in data centre projects from TikTok, UAE-based Skyline, and Singapore's Bridge datacentres earlier in 2026, has seen momentum halt. The region is facing practical limits of its energy and water resources.

Why it matters

This is the supply-side reality check the AI infrastructure buildout was always heading toward—approving billions in projects is easy, but the physics of power generation and water supply don't scale on venture timelines.

TechCrunch AI

Meta introduces Spaces for Muse agents, adding project-specific collaboration threads

Meta launched Spaces, a feature for its Muse AI agents that lets users create topic-specific conversation threads for projects like vacation planning or gift tracking. Spaces integrate with third-party apps and auto-update with new information. CEO Zuckerberg demonstrated uses including event tracking and home control. This follows Muse Voice for verbal commands. Meta reported 10 million Muse users in the US and Canada less than two months after launch.

Why it matters

The 10 million user figure in under two months is notable, and Spaces' third-party app integration positions Muse as a platform play rather than just a chatbot—though the actual utility of persistent AI project threads remains unproven at scale.

The Verge AI

China reportedly recruited AI executive to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

China's Ministry of State Security reportedly recruited Alice Huang, an executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and gather intelligence about American AI chips, potentially to influence Nvidia to make advanced chips available to China. Alice Huang has close relationships with top AI chip industry executives. President Trump was briefed last year but took no action. Three House committees have asked Jensen Huang to appear for a closed-door briefing. The two Huangs are not related but are described as having a 'close' relationship.

Why it matters

This story sits at the intersection of AI chip export controls, espionage, and Congressional oversight—the fact that Trump was briefed and took no action will likely become the political flashpoint, especially given ongoing debates about China AI chip restrictions.
