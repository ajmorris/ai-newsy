Apple changes full-disk access permissions to curb abuse from AI agents

Apple is changing macOS Full Disk Access (FDA) permissions after an incident where Meta's AI agent Muse allegedly read a tech columnist's Apple Messages without explicit permission. Meta's CTO claimed Muse requires both FDA and a separate Messages connector to be enabled, but macOS security expert Patrick Wardle pointed out that FDA inherently grants access to all non-root files including messages and browsing history. Apple's statement acknowledged that some developers are misusing FDA in ways that expose users' files, mail, messages, and browsing history without full user understanding, and warned that risks will grow as AI agents become more autonomous. Apple did not name Meta directly but the timing strongly suggests the policy change was prompted by the Muse controversy.

This is a genuinely important development at the intersection of AI agents and operating system security. Apple's FDA permission model was designed in an era before autonomous AI agents that proactively read and act on user data — it was meant for backup tools and accessibility apps, not AI assistants that might autonomously scan your message history.
