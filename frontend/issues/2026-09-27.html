Blue Cross Blue Shield Association reports that hospitals' use of AI tools in insurance claim submissions led to an additional $942 million in healthcare spending over two years. Their analysis found a sharp increase in patients being documented as having complex conditions, but no corresponding change in actual care delivered — suggesting AI is being used to upcode claims rather than improve treatment. The New York Times framed this as part of a broader trend of AI escalating the existing conflict between hospitals and insurers over payments. Abridge founder Dr. Shiv Rao acknowledged the risk of a 'bots fighting bots' dystopia but suggested AI could also reduce tensions. BCBSA's Luke Chalker called it 'a completely one-sided blood bath' favoring hospitals.

Why it matters

This is a significant early example of AI creating perverse incentives in a major industry. The core issue — AI-assisted upcoding — is essentially using technology to extract more money from the system without improving patient outcomes, which is exactly the kind of misaligned AI deployment that critics have warned about.
