Ars Technica AI

Actively exploited Plex Media Server vulnerabilities enable remote code execution

Recently patched Plex Media Server vulnerabilities are being actively exploited in the wild. A researcher developed 'Plex2Shell,' an exploit chain combining four CVEs (one rated 8.6/10) to achieve remote code execution via unauthenticated SSRF, loopback pivot, security token theft, and payload delivery. A honeypot detected active scanning and successful infections within a day, with attackers installing cryptominers and residential proxy software. The vulnerabilities were fixed in Plex version 1.43.3 released at the start of last month. The original researcher noted heavy use of AI in developing the exploit chain, though emphasized hours of iterative human-guided work were required.

Why it matters

A timely reminder that media servers exposed to the internet are high-value targets—and that AI is accelerating the exploit development timeline, making prompt patching even more critical.

Guardian AI

AI-generated actors edge toward Hollywood film roles

Two notable cases highlight the growing use of AI-generated actors in Hollywood: a digital recreation of young Val Kilmer starring in the western 'As Deep as the Grave,' and an entirely fictional AI-created actress named Tilly Norwood set for her feature film debut. Industry experts remain divided on whether AI actors replacing or supplementing real performers is imminent or still far off, with questions about the uncanny valley effect, actors' rights, and broader implications for the film industry.

Why it matters

The Val Kilmer recreation and fictional AI actress represent two very different propositions—resurrecting a consenting actor's likeness versus creating synthetic performers wholesale—and each raises distinct ethical and legal questions the industry hasn't resolved.

OpenAI

OpenAI reports major gains in AI coding for COBOL, Fortran, and other low-resource languages

OpenAI describes improvements to their models for low-resource programming languages like COBOL, Fortran, and Visual Basic. They introduce two evaluations: MultiLangBench (building a SQLite-compatible database engine in 20 languages) and a COBOL migration benchmark. GPT-6 Astra reaches 87.4% average test pass rate on MultiLangBench (up from 26.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol), and GPT-6.1 Sol achieves 97.9% accuracy on COBOL migration at high reasoning effort (up from 5.4%). They emphasize these languages underpin critical infrastructure in banking, weather forecasting, and nuclear safety.

Why it matters

Potentially one of the highest-impact practical applications of AI coding—the organizations most desperate for help maintaining legacy COBOL and Fortran systems are exactly those least able to recruit developers who know those languages.

TechCrunch AI

NYC Mayor Mamdani urges local resistance to AI, criticizes federal 'abdication' on regulation

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, speaking at an AI Now Institute event with activist Ilana Glazer, urged New Yorkers to resist the narrative that AI's takeover of jobs, education, and daily life is inevitable. He criticized the federal government's 'complete abdication' on AI regulation and its framing of AI as an arms race with China. Mamdani pointed to his recent actions—banning AI in elementary and middle schools and requiring disclosure of AI-generated real estate listings—as examples of local pushback. Why it matters

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark declared at the IMF and World Bank annual meetings that the technological singularity has begun, citing AI benchmark scores on Humanity's Last Exam jumping from 8% to 67.7% and FrontierMath from 2% to 94% since 2025. The Verge AI

US fossil fuel infrastructure sees biggest buildout in a decade as data center demand surges

The US is building more fossil fuel infrastructure than it has in a decade, driven by surging electricity demand from data centers, factories, and EVs. Renewable energy growth is slowing under the Trump administration's policies favoring fossil fuels. A report by the Environmental Defense Fund and Atlas Public Policy warns that completing planned gas projects could lock in higher pollution and electricity costs for decades. Despite renewables gaining ground—wind and solar surpassed coal for the first time in 2024, and solar accounted for 81% of new capacity—the current policy environment is shifting America's energy landscape back toward fossil fuels.

Why it matters

The irony of AI's enormous energy appetite potentially undoing climate progress deserves more attention—data centers are now one of the largest drivers of new fossil fuel demand at exactly the moment renewables were gaining dominance.

Ars Technica AI

ReactOS reaches milestone with direct NTFS installation support

ReactOS, the open-source project aiming for binary compatibility with Windows Server 2003, has enabled direct installation onto NTFS partitions for the first time. Previously limited to FAT32, the project added read-only NTFS support in 2016 and bootloader NTFS reading in 2022. The new driver still lacks caching, journaling, and security support, and may contain bugs. The volunteer-driven project, which has been in alpha for decades, sees this as an important step toward beta status, though the team acknowledges ReactOS may never be a fully viable Windows replacement.

Why it matters

A genuinely impressive achievement for a volunteer project, even if ReactOS's practical relevance remains limited—the NTFS milestone removes one of the most glaring gaps that made the project feel perpetually incomplete.

Guardian AI

Guardian column: AI is creating floods of low-quality 'slop' that cost more time than they save

Guardian columnist Gaby Hinsliff argues that AI, rather than saving time as promised, is generating an overwhelming flood of low-quality content ('slop') that costs everyone more time to deal with. She critiques the UK government's enthusiasm for transforming public services with AI, suggesting that the technology creates new burdens—from overflowing inboxes to machine-generated bureaucratic noise—that offset any efficiency gains.

Why it matters

The 'slop' framing resonates because it captures a real externality: even when AI saves the sender time, it often shifts work onto every recipient who must now filter through AI-generated volume.

TechCrunch AI

Hugging Face policy chief warns embedded AI evaluators face major hurdles

Hugging Face's chief policy officer Irene Solaiman argued at TechCrunch Disrupt that embedded AI evaluators—third-party observers placed inside AI labs as an alternative to government regulation—face significant challenges. She highlighted that model benchmarking lacks reproducibility and standardization, the evaluation field is an 'evolving science' where tests become ineffective quickly, and agentic evaluations are expensive and underdeveloped. The push for embedded evaluators has been controversial, with Anthropic's hiring of Accenture as its first evaluator drawing criticism.

Why it matters

Solaiman's critique cuts to the heart of the self-regulation debate—if we can't even standardize what 'safe' means in reproducible benchmarks, embedding evaluators inside labs risks creating accountability theater rather than real oversight.

Illinois Governor Pritzker says AI could extend lifespans to 100 or threaten humanity like nukes

Illinois Governor JB Pritzker discussed AI on the Decoder podcast, expressing optimism that AI could help extend human lifespans to 100 years while warning it poses existential threats comparable to nuclear weapons. He argued that AI regulation, privacy, and data center policy are collective action problems requiring government intervention.
