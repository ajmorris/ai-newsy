AI designs viruses never seen in nature

Stanford and Arc Institute researchers used AI models (Evo 1 and Evo 2) trained on millions of genomes to design 16 novel viruses not found in nature, which were then synthesized and shown to be viable in the lab. These AI-designed phages target only E. coli bacteria, with some replicating faster than natural counterparts. A cocktail of these AI-made viruses successfully eliminated drug-resistant E. coli, demonstrating potential for treating antibiotic-resistant infections. The researchers deliberately avoided training the model on viruses that infect humans, animals, or plants. However, since Evo 2 is open source, concerns exist about the technology being repurposed to create dangerous pathogens, raising calls for biosafety guardrails.
