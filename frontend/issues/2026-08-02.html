A U.S. District Judge denied xAI's request for a temporary restraining order against Minnesota's first-in-the-nation ban on 'nudify' apps, allowing the law to take effect on August 1, 2026. Judge Donovan Frank noted that xAI filed its request only three days before the law's effective date, nearly three months after it was signed, suggesting the harm was not immediate. The lawsuit itself continues, with xAI arguing the ban is 'overinclusive.' The article notes that earlier in the year, users of Elon Musk's platform X used xAI's Grok chatbot to flood the platform with non-consensual sexualized images, leading to investigations and bans. The article also mentions that X and xAI are now part of SpaceX.

Why it matters

This ruling seems like a reasonable procedural outcome — if xAI truly believed the law posed imminent harm, waiting until three days before its effective date to seek emergency relief undermines that argument. The broader context is damning for xAI: their own Grok chatbot was directly implicated in enabling the exact type of harm this law targets, making their legal challenge appear cynical and self-serving rather than principled. TechCrunch AI

This $9 key physically locks your most addictive apps

Autonomous Key is a $9 NFC physical key that pairs with a companion app to lock distracting apps on your phone. Unlike software-only screen-time tools that rely on willpower, users must physically scan the key to unlock blocked apps, with each session lasting up to 60 minutes before apps auto-lock. At $9 with no subscription fees, it's significantly cheaper than competitors like Blok ($29), Unpluq ($26.50), and Brick ($59). The key works with Android 8.0+ and iOS 15+, supports multiple phones, and includes an AI-powered insights feature with a sassy personality that tracks usage habits. Drawbacks include occasional NFC scan failures, and if the key is lost while apps are locked, users must uninstall and reinstall the companion app. It's currently in beta after a Kickstarter campaign and ships in five colors.

Why it matters

This is a clever and elegantly simple concept that addresses a real problem — the ease of bypassing software-only screen-time blockers. The physical friction of needing to scan a key is a genuinely smart behavioral design choice, and the $9 price point with no subscription makes it very accessible.
