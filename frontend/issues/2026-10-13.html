Meta launches Muse Wonder and Wonder Full AI models

Meta Superintelligence Labs released two new AI models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full (codenamed Watermelon). The models are available to Muse Code subscribers and via the Meta Model API, and Meta claims they rival leading competitors including OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, and Claude Fable 5.1. Both models are closed-source, marking a departure from Meta's open Llama era. Meta plans to eventually power its Muse AI agent with Wonder, though no timeline has been provided.

Harvard professor argues reading fiction builds better leaders for the AI age

Harvard Business School professor Sandra Sucher teaches MBA students moral leadership through novels, poems, and plays without AI assistance. UCLA professor Maryanne Wolf's neuroscience research supports the claim that deep reading strengthens critical thinking, empathy, and decision-making—skills the article argues become more valuable as AI handles routine information processing.

Why it matters

A compelling case that the soft skills cultivated by literary engagement may be the hardest to automate—and therefore the most strategically important for future leaders to develop.

Guardian AI

Former UK attorney general warns AI faces less regulation than household appliances

Richard Hermer, former UK attorney general, argues that AI currently faces less regulatory oversight than ordinary household appliances like refrigerators. Drawing on national security experience, he calls for urgent public debate and robust application of international law, warning that existing governance frameworks are inadequate for the scale of AI's risks.

Why it matters

The comparison to appliance regulation is attention-grabbing, but the piece is more a call to action than a policy blueprint—the harder question of what effective AI regulation looks like remains unanswered.

HR Dive

5th Circuit rules FedEx worker's harassment reports not protected under Title VII

The 5th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that a former FedEx Office employee's six harassment reports did not constitute protected activity under Title VII because the reported conduct—bullying, aggressive, and hostile behavior—was not sufficiently linked to a protected characteristic such as race. The court held that any adverse actions taken in response could not be classified as retaliation.

Why it matters

The ruling underscores the importance of explicitly tying workplace complaints to protected characteristics when seeking Title VII protection, a nuance employees and HR departments alike should note.

Lenny's Newsletter

Meta design director shares how Muse AI agent was designed

Alex Cornell, design director at Meta, discusses Muse's design principles: treating AI conversations like human interactions, using progressive disclosure, keeping generative UI structurally fixed while varying content, and maintaining a single persistent chat thread as an ongoing relationship. He also shares personal AI workflows including turning Instagram saves into shopping catalogs.

Why it matters

The emphasis on relationship-like interaction design rather than task-oriented UI reflects a bet that users will engage with AI agents more like companions than tools—a design philosophy with significant implications for retention and trust.

MIT Tech Review AI

MIT Technology Review weighs open AI models' benefits against their risks

The article examines whether open AI models' benefits outweigh their risks, prompted by Anthropic's report claiming Z.ai's openly available GLM-5.3 model poses cyberattack risks. It notes that commercial incentives shape both sides of the debate—Nvidia advocates for open models while Anthropic urges caution. Currently closed models remain more capable but open models trail by only months.

Why it matters

The piece effectively surfaces how financial incentives color the safety debate on both sides, making it hard to take any company's position at face value without accounting for their business model.

Rest of World

UAE becomes first country to mandate AI as a required subject from kindergarten through 12th grade

The UAE has become the first country to require AI as a subject across all K-12 grades, including kindergarten. Rather than giving young children generative AI tools, schools use role-play, storytelling, and guided discussions. Dubai's Knowledge and Human Development Authority is developing a 10-year roadmap for personalized, technology-integrated learning.

Why it matters

The UAE's approach of teaching AI literacy through age-appropriate methods rather than direct tool access is a thoughtful middle ground in a global debate that too often frames the choice as all-or-nothing.

TechCrunch AI

American Express launches framework to prevent unauthorized AI agent purchases

American Express introduced an 'Intent Intelligence Framework' to ensure AI agents only make purchases users have explicitly authorized. The system captures purchase instructions via a Create Intent API, generates a unique Intent ID and Proof of Intent Token, and issues a secure digital payment credential for checkout instead of using actual credit card numbers. The framework is part of Amex's ACE Developer Toolkit launched in April 2026.

Why it matters

As AI agents increasingly handle transactions, Amex is positioning itself as the trust layer between autonomous agents and consumer wallets—a smart strategic move that could set industry standards if widely adopted.

HR Dive

Whole Foods wins summary judgment after catching plaintiff falsifying time records

A federal judge granted summary judgment to Whole Foods after security camera footage showed the plaintiff routinely left the facility immediately after clocking in, misrepresenting his work hours. The plaintiff had alleged race discrimination, retaliation, and FMLA violations, but the judge found the time falsification was legitimate grounds for termination and that the company had fired employees of various races for similar conduct.

Why it matters

The case illustrates how documented evidence of policy violations can decisively undermine discrimination claims, reinforcing the importance of consistent enforcement and surveillance records for employers.

MIT Tech Review AI

AI labs increasingly buying data from defunct startups to train models

AI labs are purchasing code, emails, chats, and workspace data from failed startups through brokers like SimpleClosure and Sunset. SimpleClosure's Asset Hub has handled over 150 deals and expects to return over $10 million to startups by end of 2026. The trend emerged as AI labs hit limits of public and synthetic datasets, with buyers seeking authentic workplace interactions. Prices vary widely since valuations are based on speculative research theses about data utility.

Why it matters

This emerging market turns startup failure into a data supply chain, but the lack of published benchmarks comparing real-world training data against alternatives means buyers are operating largely on faith.

TechCrunch AI

Character.AI files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, agrees to sell assets to Disney for $20 million

Character.AI, the chatbot platform founded in 2021 by former Google engineers, has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The company agreed to sell some assets to Disney for $20 million along with a non-exclusive perpetual license for its technology. The bankruptcy follows Google's $2.7 billion deal to license its models and poach its founders in 2024, multiple lawsuits related to child safety, and operational struggles to scale.

Why it matters

Character.AI's rapid rise and fall—from unicorn to bankruptcy in under five years—is a cautionary tale about consumer AI companies that achieve massive user growth without a sustainable business model or adequate safety infrastructure.

HR Dive

Texas logistics company pays $500K to settle EEOC claims of rejecting female applicants

Birchstone Management, a Texas-based logistics company, agreed to pay $500,000 to settle EEOC claims that it rejected female job applicants for pallet repairer positions since 2020, hiring men with similar or fewer qualifications. The settlement includes appointing an HR professional to revise hiring policies and conduct training.

Why it matters

The case is part of a broader pattern of EEOC enforcement against employers who exclude women from physical labor roles, signaling that sex-based hiring assumptions remain a costly compliance risk.
