Microsoft discloses Azure and OpenAI revenue for the first time, showing $33.2B and $10B respectively

Alphabet reports $127.6B quarterly revenue but Google Search ad growth decelerates, shares fall 8%

Alphabet reported $127.6 billion in Q3 2026 revenue, up 25% year-over-year, but Google Search ad revenue growth decelerated to 12% YoY from 17% in Q2 and 19% in Q1. Shares fell 8%. Google is expanding ads within AI-powered Search experiences to international markets for the first time. CEO Sundar Pichai said Search queries are growing at their fastest rate in five years, with AI Mode queries averaging three times longer than traditional searches. Google Cloud revenue surged 103% to $30.7 billion. The Gemini app has reached 1 billion monthly users.

Why it matters

The 8% share drop despite 25% revenue growth tells the story: investors are watching the Search ad growth deceleration as a proxy for whether AI cannibalizes Google's core cash machine. The international AI Search ads expansion looks like Google's attempt to prove it can monetize AI Mode before the slowdown becomes a rout.

The Verge AI

Arizona residents mobilize against data center water consumption as Colorado River allocation shrinks

A bipartisan grassroots coalition of Arizona residents is opposing new data center developments over water usage concerns. TSMC's chip factory near Phoenix consumes nearly 5 million gallons of water daily from the municipal supply, and Arizona now hosts 172 hyperscale data centers. The opposition is driven by Arizona's looming loss of nearly 30% of its Colorado River water allocation, making tech infrastructure water demands increasingly untenable. Residents have become self-taught experts on zoning, land use, and water consumption.

Why it matters

This is the water version of the power-grid strain story that has defined AI infrastructure expansion. Arizona's situation is particularly acute because the Colorado River cuts are already locked in — this isn't hypothetical scarcity, it's arithmetic. The bipartisan nature of the opposition suggests this could become a genuine political constraint on data center siting in the American Southwest.

Instagram's Mosseri says public negativity toward AI 'going to get worse' over next two years

Instagram head Adam Mosseri said in a podcast interview that public negativity toward AI is growing and will worsen over the next couple of years, driven by anxiety, frustration, and anger — particularly in creative industries. He admitted Instagram probably doesn't rank AI content as well as non-AI content and acknowledged 'AI slop' is a real issue, though he thinks the term is used 'too liberally.' Instagram has been cracking down on AI-generated profiles and launching labels for AI accounts, though issues like celebrity deepfakes persist.

Why it matters

Mosseri's candor is notable coming from a platform aggressively integrating AI features. His framing of concerns as 'fear of being left behind' rather than legitimate critiques of quality degradation may itself fuel the negativity he's predicting.

Akai releases open-source MPC Sample Key, embracing vibe-coded mods built with AI

Akai is releasing the MPC Sample Key, a 37-key keyboard version of its budget MPC Sample, with open-source firmware. The company gave developers early access before the formal announcement, and the modding community has already built various synth mods — some using Claude. Human psychics push back against AI-generated readings, citing lack of empathy

Human psychics are pushing back against AI-generated readings, arguing that computer-generated divination lacks the empathy and personal connection that human practitioners provide. AI psychic chatbots like 'Urban Mystic' offer automated readings about auras, past lives, and predictions, while traditional practitioners warn these miss the interpersonal element central to their craft.

Why it matters

This is a colorful entry in the broader 'AI displaces human practitioners' narrative. The irony of psychics arguing for the irreplaceable human element in an evidence-free practice aside, their core complaint — that AI lacks genuine empathy — echoes concerns from therapists, counselors, and other fields where the human relationship is arguably the product.

MIT Tech Review AI

MIT Tech Review report finds only 16% of organizations extract transformative value from AI

An MIT Technology Review Insights report, produced with Atlan, finds that while all surveyed organizations use AI, only 16% extract transformative value. The largest group (40%) sees only moderate gains. The report argues the missing piece is a 'context layer' — infrastructure between business systems and AI platforms that translates enterprise intelligence into governed, machine-usable context. Most companies remain in pilot phases (38%) or using AI as assistants (35%), with only 12% running AI agents in production and 3% using AI for most work in key processes.

Why it matters

The finding that 100% of organizations use AI but only 16% get transformative value is the enterprise AI story in one stat. The 'context layer' framing is useful but unsurprising coming from a report sponsored by a metadata platform vendor. The real insight is the 3% figure for deep AI integration — the gap between AI adoption and AI transformation remains enormous.

Documentary 'Hills and Mountains' recounts Afghan woman's story of forced marriage amid Soviet invasion

'Hills and Mountains,' an 8-minute documentary directed by Afghan-born Canadian filmmaker Salar Pashtoonyar, tells the story of an Afghan woman born in 1969 whose life was transformed by the Soviet invasion in 1979. The film recounts how the invasion turned her homeland from 'peaceful and tolerant' to desperate, leading families to marry off teenage daughters to prevent starvation. Her own forced marriage followed a false accusation of an affair. The filmmaker uses landscapes of Afghanistan's mountains to connect geography, personal experience, and global history.

Why it matters

A small, personal documentary that grounds sweeping geopolitical history in one woman's experience. The choice to use a voice actor and altered details to protect her identity underscores how dangerous these stories remain to tell, decades later.

Pew Research Center

Pew finds divergent religiosity patterns among youngest U.S. adults versus slightly older peers

A Pew Research Center analysis finds mixed signals about religion among young U.S. adults. The very youngest adults (ages 18-22, born 2004-2008) report relatively high rates of religious service attendance. However, slightly older young adults (ages 23-26, born 2000-2003) are the least religious age group by measures of prayer frequency, importance of religion, religious identification, and monthly attendance.

Why it matters

The divergence between 18-22 and 23-26 year-olds is intriguing — it could reflect a genuine generational shift, a life-stage effect where college-age adults temporarily re-engage, or simply noise in a narrow age band. Worth watching whether the younger cohort maintains its relative religiosity as it ages.

Pew Research Center

Pew tracking study finds most Americans' individual religious lives held steady from 2020 to 2025

A Pew Research Center longitudinal study tracking 5,835 U.S. adults from 2020 to 2025 finds that most Americans' religious lives remained stable at the individual level. Religious affiliation, prayer frequency, and service attendance largely held steady. The study was designed to test whether stable national statistics mask offsetting individual changes, and concluded that for the majority, there was no significant shift in key religiousness indicators.

Why it matters

This is methodologically significant: it confirms that national-level stability in religious metrics reflects genuine individual consistency rather than masking equal and opposite movements. The implication is that secularization in the U.S. is primarily a cohort-replacement story — each new generation is less religious, rather than existing adults drifting away.
