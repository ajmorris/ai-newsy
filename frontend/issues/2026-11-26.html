The Verge AI

42 state AGs fight to block Character.AI's auction of users' private chats

A bipartisan coalition of 42 state attorneys general is trying to block Character.AI from auctioning over 21 million users' chat histories, including 2.1 million minors' accounts, during its Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The leading bidder was incorporated just days before being selected, with no disclosed parent company. The AGs argue the sale lacks safeguards: no requirement to honor existing privacy policies, no user data deletion mechanism, and no limits on further resale.

Update

Where coverage differs: TechCrunch reports the shell company was 'formed one day before being selected'; The Verge says it was 'only incorporated on November 13, 2026'

Why it matters

The mystery bidder detail makes this increasingly suspicious — a shell company formed the day before selection, bidding on intimate conversations of millions of minors, with no transparency requirements. This may become a landmark case for data rights in bankruptcy. TechCrunch AI

Anthropic's record $81.3B IPO coincides with GTA VI launch, the last monument to pre-AI game development

On November 26, 2026, Anthropic's $81.3 billion IPO — the largest ever — coincided with the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI, which earned $3.79 billion on release day and became the highest-rated game on Metacritic. GTA VI was made entirely without generative AI by roughly 5,000 people over 13 years, with 30,000 unique handcrafted NPCs. The piece frames GTA VI as likely the last game of its kind, a monument to human craftsmanship at the dawn of the AI efficiency era.

Why it matters

A striking symbolic coincidence. The juxtaposition powerfully illustrates the tension between efficiency-driven AI and painstaking human artistry — and raises the question of whether audiences will eventually notice or care about the difference.

TechCrunch AI

S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and 'key man' risks after Huang turmoil

S&P Global Ratings is considering downgrading Nvidia's credit rating from AA to AA- due to governance concerns following CEO Jensen Huang's ouster and reinstatement. The agency cites weak executive oversight, lack of succession planning, and heavy reliance on Huang. A decision is expected within 90 days. A downgrade could increase borrowing costs for Nvidia and for neoclouds and infrastructure companies whose debt Nvidia backstops, as Nvidia has arranged $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing where lenders were already pushing for larger guarantees.

Why it matters

The ripple effects matter more than the one-notch downgrade itself — Nvidia's $500B in infrastructure financing means its credit rating is load-bearing for the entire AI buildout supply chain.

TechCrunch AI

OpenAI merges Chat and Work into a single ChatGPT interface with shared usage limits

OpenAI is merging its Chat and Work experiences in ChatGPT into a unified interface with a shared usage meter, rolling out first to Pro subscribers with broader availability in December. The company acknowledges its heaviest users may hit limits sooner, though Instant model conversations remain unlimited. Some subscribers are concerned the merger reduces access to advanced reasoning models. The move mirrors Anthropic's merger of Claude chat and Cowork interfaces in September.

Why it matters

Framing a consolidation that may reduce power users' effective access as a UX improvement is a bold choice. The subscriber backlash will be worth watching, especially since the pricing hasn't changed.

The Verge AI

Google patent describes system for generating personalized AI video feeds and ads

Google filed a patent for 'My Channel,' a system that would generate personalized AI video feeds and advertisements using demographic data, viewing history, IP addresses, GPS locations, age, and contextual signals. The system would use LLMs to create prompts and generate tailored AI videos — including entire movies — on Google's servers. The patent has been rejected but Google could appeal or resubmit. It was filed in May 2025 and published last week.

Why it matters

Even as a rejected patent, it's a revealing look at where Google's product thinking is headed — fully synthetic, hyper-personalized content streams that blur the line between entertainment and advertising in ways that make today's algorithmic feeds look quaint.

Ars Technica AI

Backblaze reveals it has been sharing customer data with advertisers since December 2025

Backblaze's new privacy policy, effective December 1, 2026, reveals the company has been collecting and sharing customer personal information — including identifiers, commercial information, geolocation, and internet activity — with advertising and marketing partners since December 2025. Backblaze told Ars Technica the policy's table format reads more broadly than intended and that disclosures relate to website and marketing cookies, not customer backup files. The company says its legal team will review the table for clarity.

Why it matters

A cloud backup company quietly sharing customer data with advertisers for a year before updating its privacy policy is exactly the kind of trust erosion that drives customers to competitors — especially in a category where trust is the entire product.
