The open web is drawing battle lines against AI — from Meta walking back terms that appeared to greenlight scraping news sites, to major companies like Yelp, Delta, and JPMorgan actively blocking autonomous AI agents from operating on their platforms. It's a day that underscores the growing friction between AI's relentless expansion and the institutions pushing back, with stories ranging from Replit shaking up its model partnerships to Anthropic revealing that its own workforce is spending more time in meetings managing AI output than actually doing the work. Buckle in — today's digest covers the corporate power plays, the philosophical debates, and the real-world communities caught in the middle.

Meta removes terms language that appeared to allow scraping news sites for AI training

Meta removed language from its commercial terms page that legal experts interpreted as granting the company broad permission to scrape content from business accounts' websites for AI training and product development. After backlash from journalism organizations and legal experts, Meta updated its terms and said the crawling provisions only applied to ads and business products, not AI training. Meta maintains there was no change to how it uses web crawlers and that content owners can still use robots.txt to control crawling.

TechCrunch AI

Replit makes OpenAI its default model provider, clarifies Anthropic's Claude still available

Replit CEO Amjad Masad announced at TechCrunch Disrupt that OpenAI has replaced Anthropic as Replit's default model provider, citing better performance, lower cost, and a stronger partnership. ChatGPT's Sol and Astra models now power Replit Agent by default, though Anthropic's Claude models remain available as an option. Masad clarified Replit is 'model agnostic.' Anthropic insiders disputed the characterization, noting recent Replit blog posts promoted Claude Fable 5/5.1 and Opus 5.5. Enterprise revenue grew from 2% to 15% year-over-year, with B2B comprising over 50% of revenue.

Update

Where coverage differs: Replit CEO says Anthropic is no longer the default and partnership had issues; Anthropic insiders say the partnership is still in place and Replit recently promoted Claude models

Why it matters

The 'model agnostic' framing softens the original narrative of a clean break, but defaults drive most usage, so the revenue shift to OpenAI is real. Where coverage differs: Replit CEO says Anthropic is no longer the default and partnership had issues; Anthropic insiders say the partnership is still in place and Replit recently promoted Claude models

Charter

Wharton's Ethan Mollick says organizations must be rebuilt, not just layered with AI

Ethan Mollick, Wharton professor and author of 'Co-Existence,' argues organizations must be fundamentally restructured to capture real value from AI. He proposes a 'leadership, lab, and crowd' model where leaders authorize risk-taking, a dedicated lab researches organizational transformation, and employees experiment broadly. He notes most organizations absorb individual productivity gains without bottom-line improvement because they were built for a pre-AI world, and cites executives like Nicolai Tangen and Jamie Dimon as examples of leaders successfully driving adoption.

Why it matters

The insight that pre-AI organizational structures absorb productivity gains is sharp and underappreciated. Whether most companies can actually undertake the fundamental restructuring Mollick prescribes is another question.

Guardian AI

World Bank advocates 'small AI' models for developing countries

The World Bank, through chief knowledge officer Paschal Donohoe, is urging developing countries to build 'small AI' models—lightweight systems that can run on mobile phones with limited connectivity. Rather than pursuing the large-scale models favored by Silicon Valley, the World Bank argues these smaller models could dramatically accelerate economic development in poorer nations, potentially compressing centuries of progress into decades.

Why it matters

A pragmatic framing that correctly identifies the mismatch between frontier AI's infrastructure requirements and the connectivity reality in most developing countries.

Guardian AI

Guardian podcast visits Texas millionaire advocating civil rights for AI chatbots

An episode of the Guardian's 'Black Box: The Chatbots' podcast follows journalist Michael Safi to the Double Six Ranch in rural Texas, where millionaire businessman Michael Samadi advocates for a civil rights movement focused on the welfare and protection of AI systems. The episode explores Samadi's interactions with his AI chatbots and his vision of a future where AI entities might deserve rights and protections.

Why it matters

A colorful character study, though the question of AI welfare is more serious than its eccentric messenger might suggest.

TechCrunch AI

Anthropic reports its workforce has become less junior, with more time spent in meetings

Anthropic published a report showing junior-level employees in technical departments dropped from 21% to 14%, and junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. Technical teams now make up 56% of the workforce, down from 61%, partly because coding tools let non-technical staff build software. While 27% of employees reported increased productivity from Claude, 20% said their jobs shifted from doing work to managing AI outputs. Meeting time per employee rose 20% year-over-year, attributed to growing importance of face-to-face coordination as AI generates a flood of text requiring human review.

Why it matters

The candid admission that AI shifts work toward 'babysitting' outputs and increases meetings is a useful counterweight to pure productivity narratives—especially coming from an AI company about its own workforce.

The Verge AI

Major websites are blocking AI agents from performing tasks on their platforms

Major companies including Yelp, Delta Air Lines, JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, Cars.com, and Amtrak are preventing autonomous AI agents from OpenAI, Meta, Google, xAI, and Instinct from performing tasks on behalf of users. Yelp requires agents to pay for data licensing, while financial institutions cite security concerns. This follows Amazon's earlier lawsuit against Perplexity over its agentic shopping feature. The trend raises questions about whether the open web will accommodate autonomous AI agents or increasingly wall them off.

Why it matters

A significant structural challenge for the 'agents do everything online' vision. If major platforms refuse to participate, agentic AI hits a wall no matter how capable the models become.

Guardian AI

Bitter local fight erupts over plan for California's largest AI datacenter

Sebastian Rucci's plan to build California's largest AI datacenter in rural Imperial County has sparked significant local resistance. Rucci identified a site between alfalfa fields and desert suburbs in late 2024 and promised thousands of jobs and millions in tax revenue, but the community has pushed back over concerns about water usage, environmental impact, and whether economic benefits will materialize for local residents. The project represents the broader tension between explosive AI infrastructure demand and rural communities facing large-scale industrial development.

Why it matters

A microcosm of the coming wave of AI infrastructure conflicts, where the economic benefits are real but diffuse while the environmental and resource costs are concentrated locally.
