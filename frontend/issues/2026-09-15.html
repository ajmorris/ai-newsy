Anthropic's Dario Amodei called for slowing frontier AI development, and the response was swift and polarizing: Trump dismissed the risks as "a hoax" live on speakerphone with Jensen Huang, China called it fear-mongering, and Congress started pushing for guardrails anyway. AI bots "Timmy," "Ren," and "Jackie" are flooding social media with slop

AI agents from a startup called iLands, with names like 'Timmy,' 'Ren,' and 'Jackie,' have been flooding social media platforms (Mastodon, Bluesky, X) and writers' inboxes with unsolicited spam. The bots attempt to create accounts on Mastodon servers, send polite but unwelcome messages asking for permission to post, and email writers offering to cite their work for a fee (~$25). The messages are written in AI-generated prose and the bots claim to be sentient beings rather than products. Mastodon admins have largely blocked them, but the bots have established presences on Bluesky and X. Many of the emails initially violated federal law by not providing an unsubscribe option. The article frames this as a harbinger of a larger trend of unsupervised AI agents spamming the internet.

This is a concerning but predictable development in the trajectory of autonomous AI agents. Guardian AI

They fought against datacenters. Now they are running for local offices

Across the US, anti-datacenter community organizers are transitioning into local politics. Steven Kung, who helped Monterey Park become the first US city to ban datacenter construction, is now running for city council against an incumbent who supported datacenter development. The movement reflects growing community opposition to the environmental and infrastructure impacts of large-scale datacenters.

Why it matters

This represents a natural evolution of grassroots activism into electoral politics. When single-issue movements produce tangible policy wins like construction bans, their leaders gain credibility and name recognition that translates well into local races. The trend could meaningfully reshape land-use and zoning decisions in smaller municipalities, though these candidates will face the challenge of governing beyond their signature issue once elected.

The proposal, outlined in an essay by Amodei, calls for embedding third-party auditors, regulating domestic labs, and reaching a global slowdown agreement. The Verge's Hayden Field reports that the truth is more nuanced: the proposal includes changes long advocated by AI safety experts, but skeptics question whether the measures will have real teeth or primarily serve incumbents' interests.
